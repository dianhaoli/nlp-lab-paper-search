# Sweep: Self-generated curricula, environment generation, open-endedness for harness learners

Date: 2026-11-12. Scope: learning-progress teachers, PAIRED/POET/ACCEL/OMNI-EPIC lineage, Absolute Zero / R-Zero / R-Few / Agent0 / SPICE, environment synthesis (GenEnv, EnvFactory, AWM), plus the key question: **has anyone run a self-generated curriculum where the learner is a harness / prompt / memory / program and the weights stay frozen?**

Method: read the context files; ran 8 alphaXiv discover searches (recency and default) and S2 keyword searches; pulled forward citations from S2 for GEPA (about 700), R-Few, OMNI-EPIC, Hyperagents, DarwinX, Agent0 and ActiveSaddler (551 citing papers after Jun 2026; I filtered for curriculum or task-generation together with harness, prompt or frozen-model terms, which left 117); deep-read 12 papers with get_paper_content or answer_pdf_queries (ActiveSaddler, SSR/Forging More Tasks, Recursive Harness SI for Data Synthesis, HERA, RSIAgent, Studying Without a Syllabus, Agent Self-Improvement with Frozen Weights, DAEDALUS, DarwinX, RSI-Env, Harness², GRADUS, SPADE). Every ID below appeared in a tool result.

## 1. Summary of the landscape

**Short answer to the key question: yes, and it is now crowded. Between May and Nov 2026, at least 8 papers ran a self-generated or adaptive curriculum for a frozen-weight learner (harness, prompt, memory or playbook).** They split into four strands:

1. **Adaptive selection over a real pool** (the teacher picks among existing tasks): ActiveSaddler (2610.00906; failure-pattern bandit arms with LLM-scored learning progress, also applied to GEPA), Task-CoEvolve (2608.20169; adaptive *validation* selection), GEPA-Curator (2610.37651; filters noisy tasks), SSR (2610.28815; evidence-subset racing). These cover the "learning-progress bandit / frontier band / random" baseline grid (Dan's idea #4).
2. **Self-generated practice tasks feeding a frozen memory or playbook** (no real tasks at all): PREPING (2605.13880), DAEDALUS (2610.08048; explorer calibrates difficulty against solver failures, heuristic accepted only after N_s=3 consecutive successes; within 0.6–4 MSR of its curated-task variant on AppWorld/τ²/AutomationBench), RSIAgent (2609.15364; curriculum agent, broad-then-deep, target-conditioned, frozen memory), Studying Without a Syllabus (2609.10824; task-agnostic meta-agent study; **more study budget does not reliably help**).
3. **Harness and environment co-evolution** (the generator adapts to the current harness's failures): HERA (2610.06563; *runs the exact arm structure of Dan's #3*: harness-only, task-augmented static generation, generation from H0 failures, and co-evolution), DarwinX (2608.07545; static 300 synthetic intents transfer to 1,260 real WebArena-Infinity tasks, 43.5% to 93.0%), Recursive Harness SI for Data Synthesis (2610.03548; the *generator* is the evolving harness, solver frozen).
4. **Weight-learner self-play and environment design, still advancing fast**: SPADE (2608.19197; PAIRED-style hint-based regret, a single LLM writes Gym environments, 30B scale), GRADUS (2610.27781; τ²-bench "latent syllabus" coverage ledger; 30B goes from 59% to 89%), AutoEnvScaling (2610.14163), Explorer-Zero (2610.19868), LegoEnv (2611.01834), Anchored Self-Play (2607.03523), Self-Evolve With a Reference (2610.09856), Questioning the Questions / R-Quest (2610.04299), DEO (2609.34279), Training Stability under Adaptive Curricula (2611.08787).

**What is not done** (the remaining gap for Dan): no one puts *real-n scaling, synthetic-n scaling, adaptive vs static generation, anchoring, and multiple evolvers* on one **matched-compute** grid with enough statistical power. Nor does anyone ask *whether curriculum principles that work for weight learners (frontier difficulty, regret, learning progress) transfer to harness learners at all*. The strongest empirical signals point the other way:
- SSR: existing harness evolvers (AHE, HGM, MGM, Retro) **get worse** as the real task pool grows from 40 to 200 (AHE: 63.0 at 80 tasks, 57.7 at 200). A top-40 subset chosen by an LLM judge beats all 160.
- Studying Without a Syllabus: larger study budgets do not reliably improve downstream reward (5 of 6 benchmarks flat).
- DAEDALUS: performance declines beyond about 90 sessions as memory grows.
- 2611.11342 (frozen Qwen3.5-9B): about 60% of the trace-lesson gain comes from matched no-evidence search; gains on *generated* decisions did not transfer to full τ² conversations (−1.3 pp).
- DarwinX TerminalWorld: in-loop proxy 0.505 to 1.000 versus held-out 68.3%, a 31.7-pt proxy gap.

Together these suggest **harness learners saturate on evidence bandwidth, not on data**. That makes a curriculum paper for harness learners a potential "surprise" paper rather than a "+3 pts" paper.

## 4. Critiques and limitations that recur across papers

1. **No matched-compute control for the curriculum itself.** HERA, RSIAgent, DAEDALUS and Recursive-Harness-Synthesis all spend extra generation and solver rollouts on the adaptive arm (DAEDALUS uses 6–14× the rollouts of baselines). Nobody compares "adaptive curriculum" with "same budget spent on more real tasks, more seeds, or best-of-k."
2. **Single runs and small held-out sets.** HERA (1 run, 60 pairs), DarwinX TerminalWorld (41 tasks, McNemar p=1.0), ActiveSaddler (40 TB2 test tasks, 2 runs), RSI-Env (30 AIME items). τ² retail has a 33–55% verdict-flip noise floor across identical trials (cited in 2611.11342).
3. **Proxy–truth gaps.** Generated-task gains do not transfer to real episodes (2611.11342: +4.0 on generated decisions vs −1.3 on τ² conversations); DarwinX in-loop 1.000 vs held-out 68.3%. Training-pool evaluation is common (HERA Table 2).
4. **Scaling the data or budget does not help by default.** SSR (pool growth hurts), Studying Without a Syllabus (budget flat), DAEDALUS (decline beyond 90 sessions), ESPO (prompt bloat). The cause is unclear: evidence noise, context saturation, or rule interference.
5. **Self-play drift toward unrealistic tasks** (Anchored Self-Play), invalid-question growth and diversity collapse (R-Quest). Anchoring fixes it for weights; untested for harnesses.
6. **LLM judges everywhere**: failure abstraction, task feasibility, success judgment and learning-progress scoring all rely on them. Verifier leakage and criteria exposure inflate gains (2611.08318).
7. **Single evolver per paper**, so it is unclear whether curriculum effects are optimizer-specific (SSR shows evolvers differ in pool sensitivity).
8. **Target conditioning blurs "self-generated"**: RSIAgent's curriculum sees the target query, and HERA's held-out set is built by the same pipeline.

## 5. Open gaps

- G1. **Data multiplier for frozen learners.** How many real tasks is one self-generated task worth (n_eff) for a *harness* learner, as a curve over n and across evolvers? DarwinX shows synthetic works and DAEDALUS gives one point; nobody has the curve.
- G2. **Curriculum principles across learning substrates.** Do frontier-difficulty, regret and learning-progress teachers help harness learners the way they help weight learners? SPADE lists the designer side as open; nobody tests the learner side.
- G3. **Saturation mechanism.** Why do harness learners stop benefiting from more tasks (SSR, Studying, DAEDALUS)? Candidates: evidence bandwidth, rule interference, or noise in acceptance (winner's curse).
- G4. **Goodhart dynamics when the meta-agent writes its own tasks.** This is Hyperagents' stated future work. HERA, RSI-Env and RSIAgent co-evolve or self-specify tasks, but none tracks generated-score vs ground-truth-score drift over generations.
- G5. **Anchoring for harness learners.** What real fraction is needed, and does anchoring prevent drift?
- G6. **Plasticity under curricula in text space.** Does a curriculum-trained harness lose its ability to absorb new task families (prompt bloat, memory interference)? This is the UED-pathology analog of 2611.08787.
- G7. **Ground-truth instrument.** A procedural τ-style world with a *known* latent rule graph (GRADUS-style guarded operators) would let one measure what a harness actually learned, per rule, instead of pass rate on 40–114 tasks.

## 6. Threats to Dan's ideas (topic-relevant)

- **#3 Self-generated curriculum for harness evolution: partially scooped, still alive in its measurement form.** HERA runs harness-only vs static-generated vs H0-failure-generated vs adaptive co-evolution arms; DAEDALUS runs self-generated vs curated for frozen memory; DarwinX does static synthetic-to-real; RSIAgent does a curriculum agent with frozen memory; ActiveSaddler does adaptive selection. **Still open:** the n_eff curve over n ∈ {5…200}, matched compute, multi-evolver, anchoring ratio, variance decomposition (validity, diversity, difficulty, distance-to-real), and Goodhart tracking. The framing "do self-generated curricula work for harness learners?" alone now fails K1-adjacent novelty. It must become "*when and why* they stop working" (see idea 1).
- **#4 Task-selection baselines (random / LP bandit / frontier band): largely scooped** by ActiveSaddler (LP-like bandit, +GEPA), Task-CoEvolve, GEPA-Curator and SSR. Keep only as baselines inside #3.
- **#5 Anchoring ablation:** done for weights (Anchored Self-Play, Self-Evolve With a Reference); **not done for harnesses**, so it is alive as one axis of #3.
- **#8 PAIRED-style regret generator for LLM agents: scooped** by SPADE (hint-based regret, 30B). Park it.
- **#7 Co-evolutionary Goodhart:** co-evolution is done (HERA, SPADE, Rec-Harness-Synthesis) but drift is unmeasured, so it is alive as a measurement. It can be done cheaply with frozen weights (idea 3).
- **"Unified" arm (Hyperagents writes its own tasks):** partially covered by RSI-Env (the Self improver builds its own local validation), RSIAgent and HERA. A rigorous version with ground-truth drift is still open.
- **Infra D (procedural τ-style generator):** GRADUS, AutoGym, Skill2Env, EDGEGEN, Δτ-Bench and Apple's controllable synthesis (2610.37648) crowd it. Treat it as an instrument only; borrow GRADUS's guarded-operator idea for latent-rule ground truth.
- **Scoop watch (next 6 months):** ActiveSaddler or HERA follow-ups adding generation; DAEDALUS v2 scaling; SPADE "in-context designer" follow-up; Agent Plasticity follow-ups applying plasticity to curricula.

## 7. Proposed ideas

### Idea 1: "More tasks, no gain": the data multiplier and saturation law of harness learners
- **Core question:** For frozen-weight learners (GEPA, an ACE/DAEDALUS-style memory learner, a Meta-Harness-lite code evolver), how does held-out performance scale with the number of real tasks n and of self-generated tasks m at matched rollout budget, and what sets the saturation point?
- **Claim:** For harness learners, self-generated tasks have a data multiplier n_eff/m ≥ 1 only below a saturation point n* that is set by the optimizer's evidence bandwidth (items consumed per update), not by generator quality. Past n*, adding real *or* synthetic tasks is flat or harmful, so adaptive curricula help harness learners only by *selecting*, not by *adding*.
- **Key experiment:** a procedural τ²-style world with known latent rules (GRADUS-style operators) for unlimited held-out tasks. Grid: n ∈ {5,10,30,60,120,200} real; m generated by static / adaptive (frozen writer) / anchored (5–20% real) generators; 3 evolvers × 2 open models (e.g., Qwen3.x 8–30B via cheap API); vary evidence bandwidth k ∈ {5,20,all}; ≥5 seeds; budgets matched in rollouts and tokens. Report S_real(n), S_gen(m), n_eff, and per-rule coverage. Falsifiable either way: if n* moves with k but not with generator quality, that is the claim; if it moves with generator quality, curricula are the lever.
- **Why new:** SSR shows non-monotone pool scaling for real tasks only; DAEDALUS gives one synthetic-vs-curated point; DarwinX has no real arm; nobody has the n_eff curve or the bandwidth manipulation.
- **Closest prior:** SSR 2610.28815; DAEDALUS 2610.08048; HERA 2610.06563; Studying Without a Syllabus 2609.10824.
- **Cost:** about $600–1,500 API on open models; pilot about $40.

### Idea 2: Same teacher, different learner: do curriculum principles transfer from weights to harnesses?
- **Core question:** Do the teacher signals that win for weight learners (frontier difficulty band, hint-based regret, EMA learning progress) also win for harness learners, or does the ranking flip toward coverage/diagnostic teachers (GRADUS ledger, failure-pattern arms)?
- **Claim:** Teacher rankings flip across learning substrates. Frontier-difficulty teachers maximize gains for gradient learners but are among the worst for harness learners, which need *rule coverage* and *contrastive pairs*. "Learnability at 50% pass rate" is a property of gradient estimation, not of learning in general.
- **Key experiment:** one procedural environment and 5 teachers (random, frontier band, LP bandit, hint-regret, coverage ledger) × 2 learners: (a) GEPA/ACE harness on a frozen 8B model; (b) LoRA-GRPO on the same 8B model. Matched teacher compute; 5 seeds; measure held-out pass and rule coverage. A 2-way interaction test is the headline.
- **Why new:** SPADE explicitly leaves "learned weights vs in-context evolution" open on the designer side; ActiveSaddler tests one teacher on a harness learner; nobody crosses teachers × substrates.
- **Closest prior:** SPADE 2608.19197; ActiveSaddler 2610.00906; GRADUS 2610.27781; DEO 2609.34279.
- **Cost:** about $500 API plus about 200–400 GPU-hours for small-model LoRA-GRPO (the moderate tier). Could drop arm (b) to published weight-learner rankings if the GPU budget fails, but that weakens the claim.

### Idea 3: Curriculum Goodhart: when a meta-agent writes its own tasks, its proxy and the truth diverge
- **Core question:** When the self-improving meta-agent (Hyperagents / Gödel-Agent style) also writes its training tasks, does the gap between its in-loop score on self-written tasks and ground-truth held-out performance grow with generations, and what drives it?
- **Claim:** Self-written curricula for harness learners show a characteristic Goodhart curve. Generated-task score rises monotonically while ground-truth performance peaks and declines after a few generations, driven by drift toward harness-solvable but unrealistic tasks (rule restatement, verifier-shaped tasks). Small real anchoring (≤5%) flattens the curve.
- **Key experiment:** a procedural world where ground truth is exact. Arms: fixed real; static generated; adaptive frozen writer; unified (the meta-agent edits both the harness and the task writer); unified plus anchoring. Track the proxy–truth gap, distance-to-real (embedding/rule-distribution), validity and diversity per generation over 20–40 generations, multiple seeds, matched compute. Link to the winner's-curse selection-noise model (2610.09239).
- **Why new:** HERA, RSI-Env, RSIAgent and Rec-Harness-Synthesis co-evolve or self-specify tasks but never measure drift with ground truth; 2611.11342 and DarwinX report static proxy gaps only. This is Hyperagents' stated future work, made measurable. It fits Xunjian's Gödel Agent / self-referential agenda directly.
- **Closest prior:** HERA 2610.06563; Anchored Self-Play 2607.03523; Winner's Curse 2610.09239; DarwinX 2608.07545.
- **Cost:** about $800–1,500 API (long runs), no GPU.

### Idea 4: Harness plasticity under curricula
- **Core question:** Does a harness evolved under an (adaptive) curriculum lose the ability to absorb new task families (plasticity loss in text space), and do curricula make this better or worse than real-task streams?
- **Claim:** Harness learners show plasticity loss analogous to UED learner pathologies: bloat and rule interference reduce the cost-normalized gain on a new task family. Adaptive curricula *accelerate* this loss relative to uniform streams, and simple resets (prune / consolidate / re-derive) recover it.
- **Key experiment:** sequential task families in the procedural world. Measure Agent-Plasticity-style metrics (cost-normalized held-out gain on family k+1) for harnesses at generation t versus a fresh harness; compare curricula; interventions = consolidation, random pruning, reset-to-skeleton. Multiple evolvers.
- **Why new:** Agent Plasticity (2610.08902) uses fixed streams; Training Stability under Adaptive Curricula (2611.08787) is weights-only; DAEDALUS, ESPO and SSR report symptoms without framing or measuring them as plasticity.
- **Closest prior:** 2610.08902; 2611.08787; DAEDALUS 2610.08048; SSR 2610.28815.
- **Cost:** about $500–1,000 API.

### Idea 5 (support artifact, not standalone): LatentRule-τ, a procedural world with ground-truth rule graphs
A τ²-style generator (sampled DB state + goal + composable guarded operators; verified end state) whose latent rule graph is known, so the "what was learned" metric is per-rule recall/precision of the harness, plus "distance-to-real" calibration against τ² retail and airline. It is the instrument for ideas 1–4 and fixes the power problem (τ² has a 33–55% verdict-flip noise floor). On its own it is crowded (GRADUS, AutoGym, Δτ-Bench, CLR-Bench 2610.40758).
