# Critique: Meta-plasticity (T2): "Do self-improvers lose the ability to improve?"

Reviewer stance: skeptical area chair and related-work searcher. Date: 2026-11-12.
Verdict: **viable with fixes (about 68/100)**. No kill flag fires cleanly. But nearly every *ingredient* was published between Oct and Nov 2026. The only open space is the **meta-level** version: the improver's own transferable improvement ability, a warm-vs-fresh improver on a new domain, tracked over a long horizon, with the reset question split into procedure vs memory. The paper must be framed tightly around that gap or reviewers will call it a re-run of "Scaling Discovery Requires Forgetting" plus "Continual AutoResearch".

**Scoop verdict.** No paper checkpoints a *self-referential improver* at k in {0..40} and measures its ability to improve a **fresh target on a new domain** against its generation-0 self at matched $. None ties that to improver complexity, or splits reset into procedure vs notes. However, **at least 4 papers from Oct to Nov 2026** cover (a) accumulation causing a plateau with reset restoring progress, (b) warm vs reset on a new task, (c) plasticity per $ as a metric, and (d) continual harness evolution. The N score is therefore capped around 2.5.

## 3. Mock NeurIPS review

**Summary.** The paper asks whether self-improving agents lose their ability to improve, i.e. meta-level loss of plasticity. It runs 2 to 3 improver families for 40 to 60 generations and probes warm vs fresh improvers on a new domain at several k. It regresses the penalty on complexity and tests prune and reset interventions at matched compute.

**Strengths**
- It asks a clean, falsifiable question that the self-improvement field keeps gesturing at: Gödel Agent's complexity ceiling, Meta-Harness's failure to compound, and Audit-the-Scaffold's saturation. Either sign of the result is informative.
- The Dohare-style probe (warm vs fresh at a fixed budget, as a curve over k) is the right design and imports a respected methodology from the continual-RL literature.
- Matched-compute interventions and a procedure-vs-memory split could produce actionable guidance on what an improver should keep.

**Weaknesses (as currently scoped)**
1. **Novelty is thin at the level of the headline claim.** "Accumulated history makes agents worse at improving, and resetting fixes it at matched $" is the thesis of 2610.20563 and 2610.07447. "Warm agents do not beat reset agents on new tasks" is 2611.05108. "Plasticity per $" is 2610.08902. A reviewer will say: *"this is the forgetting result moved one level up; what do we learn that those papers didn't tell us?"* That question only has an answer if the paper isolates the meta level.
2. **The meta level may not exist in practice.** SelfImprove-Bench shows HyperAgents keeps no meta edits. If the improver's code barely changes, the warm-start penalty comes from notes, archives and context, not the improver, and the "meta-plasticity" label is misleading. The paper needs a manipulation check: how much of the improver's own procedure changed by generation k.
3. **Statistical power.** PR(k) = gain_B(warm) / gain_B(fresh) is a ratio of two small, noisy gains (often 2 to 6 pp on τ-bench-like tasks). With 3 seeds, the ratio's CI will span 0 to 2. A claim like "peaks within about 10 generations then declines" needs far more resolution than 6 probe points x 3 seeds.
4. **Confounds**: warm improvers carry longer prompts and contexts, which increases cost per call. "At matched budget" must mean matched $ *including* context bloat, or the penalty is just cost. Domain distance between A and B governs interference (2611.03219), so a single A-to-B pair is uninterpretable. The selection gate also matters (2610.25858: the Meta-Harness gate freezes learning).
5. **Overspecified claim.** "Peaks within about 10 generations" and "restores at least half" are guesses, not hypotheses with theory behind them. Reviewers will treat them as post-hoc if the data come out differently.
6. **Generality**: 2 to 3 families on 1 to 2 domains invites "single benchmark, engineering". The rubric's C criterion needs an artifact: a probe protocol plus a procedural task generator.

**What makes it "workshop"**: one family, one A-to-B pair, 3 seeds, and the result "warm is a bit worse and reset helps". **What makes it "incremental"**: not citing or beating Forgetting / Fork-and-Flush as reset baselines. **What makes it main-track**: showing *where* the penalty lives (procedure vs memory vs archive), that it is specific to the meta level (it appears even when the target agent is fresh), that it scales with k in a dose-response way across families and domain distances, and that the object-level fix (forgetting) is *insufficient* or *different* at the meta level.

**Likely score as proposed**: 4 (borderline reject); confidence 4. **With fixes**: 5 to 6 (borderline to weak accept).

**Kill flags**

- K2 (no outcome interesting): not fired. Both penalty and positive meta-transfer are informative, though a noisy null is not.
- K3 (closed models): not fired if open or cheap models (DeepSeek-V4 Flash, Qwen3.5) can drive the improver. Verify in the pilot.

## 5. Fixes that raise the score

1. **Make the meta level the identification target (N +0.5 to 1, C +1).** Use a 2x2 or 2x3 *transplant probe* at each k: {gen-0 vs gen-k improver **procedure/code**} x {empty vs gen-k **notes/memory/archive**}. Each cell improves a **fresh target agent** on domain B at matched $. This separates procedural meta-plasticity loss from accumulated-memory interference, which no existing paper does. It also answers Dan's "is it the procedure or the memory?" question.
2. **Manipulation check (kills the SelfImprove-Bench threat).** Report meta-edit share per generation (diff size of the improver's own code vs the target code). Include Gödel Agent (which edits its own improve routine by construction) and DGM-H with forced meta-edit budgets, plus a **fixed-improver control** (as in SelfImprove-Bench's HyperDSH fixed improver). If the meta level does not change, say so and rescope to "what accumulation does to improvers".
3. **Use the published baselines as reset arms.** Add Forgetting (keep the best, discard everything) and Fork-and-Flush (keep the workspace, flush context) as the reset baselines, alongside the proposed procedure-reset, notes-distill, prune-to-budget and selectionist-consolidation arms. The new claim becomes: *what the improver should keep differs at the meta level from the object level*.
4. **Adopt Agent Plasticity's metric.** Report P (gain per $) and P_sat on B for warm vs fresh, plus learning-curve AUC in $. Drop the bespoke P_meta name or define it as "P of a fresh target under improver gen-k". Compute $ including context bloat.
5. **Power.** Use a procedural task generator (the τ-style generator in Dan's infrastructure lane) for unlimited fresh B instances. Use paired seeds (the same B task draws for warm and fresh) and analyse the **difference** gain_warm - gain_fresh with hierarchical bootstrap, not the ratio. Fit a dose-response in k (log k), not a "peak at 10". Pre-register: slope < 0 => penalty; slope > 0 => meta-transfer.
6. **Domain-distance axis.** Probe at least 2 B domains (near and far from A). Interference depends on relatedness (2611.03219, 2610.25858), and a single pair cannot be interpreted.
7. **Mechanism readouts borrowed from Forgetting.** Track edit size, reasoning tokens, proposal diversity and the reachable-edit-set over k for the *improver's proposals on B*. If warm improvers make smaller and less diverse edits on a fresh domain, that is a meta-level anchoring mechanism. It directly tests Audit-the-Scaffold's reachable-set claim.
8. **Scope down to save budget.** Use 2 families (Gödel Agent and a DGM-H/Hyperagents-style improver, or GEPA-style as a non-self-referential contrast), k in {0, 5, 10, 20, 40}, and 5 seeds on cheap models rather than 3 families x 60 generations x 3 seeds. Run a $50 pilot first: Gödel Agent, 20 generations, probes at k=0 and k=20 on one B domain. Check whether the signal exceeds the seed noise.
9. **Framing.** Avoid "stability-plasticity" (owned by 2610.25858) and "forgetting helps" (owned by 2610.20563). Frame as **"improvability transfer" and the warm-start penalty of self-referential improvers**, with Gödel Agent's complexity ceiling as the motivating anomaly. Keep the claim that positive meta-transfer is equally publishable. Hyperagents' unreplicated transfer result (imp@50 = 0.630) becomes the natural replication target if the sign is positive.

## 6. Sharpened headline claim

> *"After k generations of self-improvement on domain A, a self-referential improver improves a fresh agent on a new domain B less per dollar than its own generation-0 self. The penalty grows with k and is carried by the improver's accumulated **procedure**, not its notes: transplanting the gen-0 procedure while keeping the gen-k notes restores at least half of the lost gain at matched compute. History-wipe forgetting, which fixes object-level plateaus, does not restore it. If instead gen-k improvers beat gen-0 on B, improvement ability transfers, and we locate it in the procedure or the memory."*

Either branch yields a "where does improvability live" result that 2610.20563, 2611.05108 and 2610.08902 do not provide.
