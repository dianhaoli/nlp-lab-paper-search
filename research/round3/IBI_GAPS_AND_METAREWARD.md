### (1) Best meta-reward paper: one-page proposal

**Title.** *Order-0 Rewards Breed Routers, Not Improvers: Which Meta-Reward Raises Successor Quality, and Through Edit Rate or Edit Quality?*

This merges the MR-A bake-off, the MR-E headline, the MR-C hurdle machinery and the GAP-1 correlation. MR-E's RL arm becomes a stretch arm.

**Claim (two-sided, pre-registered).** Some loops select improver edits on order-0 signals: child score, matched-budget Δ0 (RSI-Harness-style) or descendant quality (Looped-RSI/CMP-style). In those loops, selection lowers the improver's meta-edit rate p and leaves held-out successor quality Δ1 unchanged, because selection favours evidence-routing edits. Two other kinds of reward instead raise Δ1, mainly through p:
- replay-family rewards (a hurdle Q1-hat, IFR-style, HMED-style);
- the order-0 reward plus a forced-edit quota.

The pre-registered estimands are ΔΔ1, Δp and Δμ between arms. Either sign is publishable. "Order-0 selection already finds order-1 edits" would directly support Looped-RSI and RSI-Harness. Do not pre-commit to any p ≥ 0.2 crossover threshold. Present the cost model as a derived hypothesis.

**Alternatives considered.**
- **MR-E as the lead (GRPO on the nested reward).** Its core is the most novel. But the judges found the base 8B p probably ≈ 0, GRPO groups with zero variance, and 1.5–2k+ GPU-hours, which is about 3× the plan. Keep it as a small 1.7B/4B arm only if the gate below passes.
- **MR-B audit.** K4-shaped, and it needs fidelity to others' code.
- **MR-C standalone.** Too thin; the hurdle estimator is textbook zero-inflated estimation.
- **MR-D.** VERSE already shows the crossing.

**Design.**
- **Testbed.** The IbI harness (Hyperagents-style operator file). The task agent evolves on child dev score. Main backbone is Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B (local vLLM or DeepInfra), plus one second family (DeepSeek-V3.1 or Llama-3.3-70B) for the key contrast only.
- **Arms**, each 5 seeds × 40 generations at matched dollars, counting modifier plus evaluation tokens:
  - A1 child score (IbI/Hyperagents default).
  - A2 Δ0 with an LSE/MetaEvolve-style no-improvement penalty.
  - A3 descendant/CMP.
  - A4 hurdle Q1-hat = p̂·μ̂ on a training meta-state pool.
  - A5 IFR-style (current gain plus a historical-gain floor).
  - A6 random promotion (negative control).
  - A7 A1 plus a forced diagnose quota every k=5 generations, with a placebo-render twin. This is GAP-3's useful part.
  - Held-out-family Q^(0) is reported only as a labelled oracle ceiling.
- **Measurement.**
  - Score improvers at generations 0, 20 and 40 by full nested replay.
  - Use 48 held-out meta-states from 3 or more fault families. These must be **disjoint** from the A4/A5 selection pool, with independent replay seeds; this breaks the Goodhart circularity all three judges flagged.
  - Use R=6.
  - The successor endpoint is a rebuilt 40-state harder bank on which M1 sits near 0.5. This removes IbI's 0.84 ceiling (gap 12).
  - Report p, μ = E[Q|edit], Δ0 and Δ1 separately.
  - Add a non-replay second endpoint: product gain after k inherited generations.
  - Edit-level analysis: corr(Δ0, Δp) over all promoted edits, with a disattenuation correction (GAP-1).
  - Classify each diff blind as routing, budget, reword or structural, with two coders plus κ.
- **Calibration gate.** Replicate IbI's positive control (+0.171) on the open backbone first. If the assay cannot detect a known Δ1 there, every null is uninterpretable.

**Power.** TOST at ±0.05 is **not** achievable, because IbI's resolution of 0.13 at 6 meta-states scales to roughly 0.065 at 24. So:
- Pre-register a minimum detectable effect from pilot variance. The target is MDE ≈ 0.06–0.08 on ΔΔ1, using a mixed model with seed (the top-level unit), meta-state and replicate as nested levels.
- Use common random numbers across arms.
- Treat p as the primary, best-powered outcome. With 288 modifier runs per improver it is cheap, which is why it leads.

**Cost.** IbI-style modifier runs cost about 1¢. Nested replay is about $3–8 per improver; 7 arms × 5 seeds × 3 checkpoints ≈ 105 improvers, or $350–800. In-loop replay selection for A4/A5 adds about $400–600. The loops and the second backbone add about $300–500. **Total ≈ $1.4–2.2k** including about 25% contingency, within the $3k cap. Re-cost from the pilot. If over budget, cut in this order: generations to 30, then arms A3/A5, then the second backbone, and never meta-states or R.

**Pilot (2 weeks, about $80).**
- Measure base p and μ for Qwen3-Coder-30B on the IbI banks (100 modifier runs).
- Replicate the positive control (7 meta-states × 6).
- Run A1 vs A4, 2 seeds × 15 generations.
- Price one nested replay end to end.

**GO** if all hold:
- base p ≥ 0.05, *or* the diagnose dose moves p across about 0.05–0.5;
- the positive control gives Δ1 > 0 with a CI excluding 0;
- split-half reliability of Q^(1) ≥ 0.6;
- per-candidate Q1-hat costs ≤ 10¢.

**KILL or PIVOT** if the positive control is undetectable on two open backbones. Then pivot to the measurement-only paper (GAP-4 or GAP-1 below), or switch to MiMo or another cheap API backbone.

**Timeline.**

- Dec: freeze the pre-registration (OSF) by about Dec 20. Build the arms.

**Closest work and delta.**
- **IbI.** Only measures Q^(1), and every loop there selects on child score.
- **IFR, HMED.** Each is a single replay-based selector, with no head-to-head comparison, no p/μ split and no nested-replay endpoint. Include both as arms.
- **RSI-Harness, Looped-RSI, MetaEvoForge, Meta-TTL.** One reward each, evaluated at product level.

- **Our delta.** The first controlled comparison of meta-rewards scored on held-out nested-replay Q^(1), with a decomposition into p and μ and a causal forced-edit placebo.

**Fast-follower risk and how to beat it.**

### (2) Comparison with Meta-Overfitting and Taught, Not Selected; what to merge

- **Taught, Not Selected stays the cleanest lead.** It is distinct from this whole cluster, untouched by IFR, HMED and IbI, and scored 73.1. The meta-reward paper is a stronger version of the *improver* line, not a replacement for Taught, Not Selected.
- **Meta-Overfitting and the meta-reward paper should merge.** Two reasons:
  1. *Meta-Overfitting's main risk is the improver edit rate*, since ZMmJDeDTXk finds improvers almost never edit themselves. The meta-reward machinery (the p measurement, the forced quota with placebo, the hurdle Q1-hat) is exactly the fix. It guarantees enough accepted improver edits to have something to transfer.
  2. *Meta-Overfitting's Decay claim becomes the held-out-domain endpoint* of the bake-off: Δ1(A→B) against Δ1(A→A) by domain distance, after memory wipe. This is gap 14.

  The merged paper reads: "which meta-reward produces improvers that are better *and* domain-general?" It is one paper with a coherent single estimand family (Q^(1) by p and μ, then by distance). Cap scope: 4 core arms (A1, A4, A6, A7) × 2 domains (near, far) if budget binds.
- **Plan.** Keep the original Week 1 Taught, Not Selected pilot (≤ $60). Replace Week 2's Meta-Overfitting edit-rate pilot with the merged pilot above. Pick the lead by the gates. If both pass, Taught, Not Selected is the safer main paper on novelty, and the merged improver paper is the stronger fit for Xunjian (Gödel Agent lineage).

### (3) Two best gap ideas, 5 lines each

**GAP-4: Auditing order-2 self-improvement claims (63.2, medium scoop risk).**
1. The target is MetaRSI's +2.3 / +5.5 "meta-update advantage" (2609.06396, one run per term) and Metaⁿ's emergent depth. Test whether either exceeds the p₁p₂·g null plus routing.
2. Experiment: a replicated MetaRSI-style term comparison on Qwen3-Coder-30B, crossing forced vs natural edits at levels 1 and 2. Report p₂ and replicate variance. Add an evidence-ablation arm at level 2.
3. The forced cell estimates g₂, not Δ2. Recover natural Δ2 = p̂₂·ĝ₂ with a delta-method CI. The positive-control modifier is a sanity check only.
4. Theory must be a tight minimax lower bound on sample complexity for unforced order-r estimation. The p^r upper bound is an IbI corollary; drop it, and drop "geometric decay" unless there is an order-3 cell.
5. Deliverable: a pre-registered futility rule (add a meta-level only if p·|g| exceeds the budget's MDE), tested on one external system. About $500. Watch the MetaRSI group.

**GAP-1: Routing suppresses meta-editing, an edit-level causal test (62.1, medium scoop risk).**
1. Estimand: the joint distribution of (Δ0, Δp, Δ1) per edit, with two-sided CIs, corr(Δ0, Δp) and P(Δ1<0 | Δ0>0). No fixed thresholds, and no "first order reversal" claim, since IFR and OnzWTehIaN have lineage-level versions.
2. The primary result is causal: insert or remove the routing clause on fixed edits across 2 backbones. The placebo is a length-matched clause that does not route. This extends IbI's 0/36 self-edit result into a mechanism.
3. Population in two strata: native improver edits (report the true count) and task edits cast in the improver role. Use family fixed effects.
4. Use disjoint target splits for Δ0 and Δ1 to avoid shared-noise reversals. Add an identity-edit control for the false-reversal rate.
5. This folds naturally into the merged paper as its mechanism section. About $300 standalone for 2–3 families; cut the 40-generation lineages.

(GAP-3 scored slightly higher, 63.3, but RSI-Harness and HMED already own its mechanism. Its useful part, the placebo quota, is already arm A7 above.)

## Earlier claims, re-checked against the full paper

1. **"The paper doesn't explain why improvers rarely self-edit": REFINED (all four lenses agree).** The paper gives a descriptive mechanism: routing improvers send effort to the task and "almost never edit the improver, even when the evidence implicates it". The counts are 0/36 and 0/72, and fixed improvers spent 150/500 and 157/300 edits on an inert copy of their own file. It also shows one sufficient cause: an explicit diagnose instruction raises edits from 11/42 to 35/42. It never manipulates the selection signal, operator-file salience, gate resolution or backbone reluctance, and it never splits Q^(1) into p and μ. The causal "why" is still open.

2. **"Forcing self-updates at matched compute over long horizons is untested": CONFIRMED (all four lenses).** The only forced meta-edit is a one-step, pilot-tuned prompt on 7 constructed meta-states, with no cost accounting. The 4-step and 80-generation results are order-0 only. Refinement: concurrent work now runs matched-budget dual loops or decoupled meta-rewards (RSI-Harness, Looped-RSI, MetaEvoForge, Meta-TTL, VERSE). Going by abstracts, none has a placebo arm, an edit-rate decomposition or nested replay. The open space is therefore "forcing with an order-1 assay", not forcing as such.

3. **"Cross-domain transfer of improver edits is untested": REFINED. The claim is wrong as stated at order 0.** T and B called it partly wrong; R and S called it refined. Reconciled: M1's order-0 gain does transfer across three coding banks (+0.38 / +0.26 / +0.30) and reappears in prospective loops on 8 disjoint families, and Hyperagents' transfer configurations are audited at archive level. What remains untested is order-1 (successor-quality) transfer, any non-coding domain, and backbone transfer at order 1. The claim should be restated that narrowly.

4. **"The openness law is untested": CONFIRMED (all four lenses).** The editable surface is one operator file, the harness fixes the budget and ignores the cap, and surface size is never varied. Released configurations with other surfaces (e.g. "improver edits parent selection") are audited only for persistence.

5. **"A reality-grounded continual benchmark is untested": CONFIRMED and STRENGTHENED.** The banks are constructed (12 states, 6 meta-states) plus SWE-bench panels of 10 and 20 instances. The paper's own prospective loops saturated: every seed reached a perfect dev score between generations 2 and 37 while 4 of 5 were still running M0. That is direct evidence that the current setup cannot show improver gains over time. Field-level continual benchmarks exist (SelfImprove-Bench, RSI-Env, Flex-RSI), but none measures order 1.

6. **"Order ≥2, Gödel Agent and other open improver families are untested": MOSTLY CONFIRMED, one part overstated (S and B flagged it; R and T agree in substance).** Order ≥2 is an explicit limitation. Gödel Agent, STOP, SICA, HGM and MetaRSI are cited but not analysed. However, DGM, ADAS and Metaⁿ histories are audited at archive and order-0 level, and the routing panel spans six backbones. ## What else the full read reveals

- **The headline null is weaker than it reads.** It comes from 6 meta-states, cut from a registered 12 before any evaluation, with no equivalence margin. The point estimate on target M0 is positive (+0.031, interval up to +0.126). On target M1, ceiling compression (0.842 on 4 states) limits what can be seen at all.
- **There is a hidden positive signal.** When M1 does edit M0, the children score 0.86, against 0.57 for M0's own edits. M1 may be a better improver per edit and a worse one per run, because it rarely edits. This hurdle split is the single cheapest re-analysis and could change the paper's story.
- **The positive control's content is routing.** Its +0.171 comes mostly from edits that send the next improver to the evaluation results. Either evidence routing is the only lever found at both orders, or the control is not a test of genuinely new meta-level competence.
- **Score-measurability is a loophole in Thm 3.** The non-identification result excludes rules that inspect operator code, which is exactly how a cheap diff- or judge-based Q^(1) predictor would work. The paper does not explore this.
- **Prop 7 has direct consequences for concurrent systems.** Descendant- and clade-based improver selection (Looped-RSI, HGM-style CMP) optimizes a different target than successor quality. The two agree only if the declared meta-state distribution equals the search-induced one, which this paper never estimates either.
- **The routing benefit depends on the backbone.** It is zero to negative for models that already read the report (DeepSeek 0.000, Gemini −0.109). Flex-RSI independently finds the improver adds 0.0 for a frontier model. Improver gains may shrink as backbones get stronger, and that has not been tested at order 1.
- **Selection rarely finds the good edit.** It found evidence routing in 1 of 5 prospective seeds, only 1 of 6 selected versions raised Q^(0), and selection stopped distinguishing versions once dev saturated. The loop itself is a weak discovery engine under child-score selection.
- **Archive-curve critique is now crowded.** Relevant papers: 2610.27610 (census of 114 RSI papers; 22% audit 2+ reruns; 4 of 26 compare improvers from identical starts), 2611.07624, Ri0rU3rPOS, QDdWiaNkk2. OnzWTehIaN covers verification theory for training-level RSI. New work should build on order-1 measurement (gaps 1–11), not on more archive-curve critique.
- **Discrepancies between lenses:** the comparison for M1's edited children is reported as 0.549 (cell mean) or 0.57 (M0's own edits), and the order-0 transfer bank is called "interface" in some lenses and "state-tracking" in others. Both details should be checked against Table 2 and App B before quoting.
- **Caveat on concurrent work:** claims about concurrent systems (RSI-Harness, Looped-RSI, MetaEvoForge, Meta-TTL, Flex-RSI and others) are based on abstracts and an OpenReview search, not full texts. Whether any of them uses a Q^(1)-like assay should be checked before positioning against them.

### Training Improvers for Successors: RL on Nested-Replay Reward vs Child Gain for a Small Open Model (`MR-E`, meta-reward) — panel 67.2/100

**Claim.** GRPO-training a 7-8B open improver model with reward equal to the edit-conditional nested-replay successor gain raises held-out Delta1 by >= 0.05 over the base model. Training on child gain (Delta0) raises Delta0 but cuts the meta-edit rate p by >= 30% and leaves Delta1 at or below zero. In this case, reward on order 0 actively trains away order-1 competence.

**If opposite.** Both rewards produce the same Delta1 (equivalence margin 0.05), or the Delta0 reward also raises p. That would show that order-0 RL already captures order-1 skill, so a separate meta-reward is unnecessary at small scale.

**Design.** The policy is Qwen3-8B (or Qwen2.5-Coder-7B) with LoRA, trained by GRPO on 2 x A100/H100 for about 3-4 days per arm. All environments run locally on the same GPUs with a frozen Qwen3-8B task agent, so the API cost is close to zero. The environment consists of IbI-style semantic and interface banks: the policy acts as the modifier and edits a target improver given meta-evidence. Reward arms: (1) Delta0, the child task score; (2) nested-replay successor gain, where the edited improver is applied to 2 inner targets and scored by unit tests; (3) the hurdle reward from MR-C, with p and mu shaped separately; (4) an untrained base. Training uses 120 meta-states from 6 fault families, and the held-out test uses 24 meta-states from 2 unseen families with 6 replicates each. We report p, mu, Delta0 and Delta1 and run TOST with |Delta1| < 0.05. There are 3 training seeds per arm. Diffs are classified to check whether the Delta0-trained policy collapses onto routing or prompt-rewording edits, i.e. reward hacking. IbI's power analysis motivates the 24 x 6 test design: at 6 meta-states the sign-flip test resolves only effects larger than about 0.13. Estimated full cost is about $150 in API for the judge plus about 600 GPU-hours.

**Pilot.** One week, about $20 in API plus about 60 GPU-hours. ### Which Meta-Reward Breeds Better Improvers? A Nested-Replay Bake-off for Decoupled Meta/Task Optimization (`MR-A`, meta-reward) — panel 65.1/100

**Claim.** In a loop that optimizes the task agent on task score and the improver separately, selecting improver edits on a cheap hurdle estimate of nested-replay successor quality (Q1-hat = p-hat x mu-hat) raises held-out Q^(1) more per dollar than child score, improvement delta, or descendant/CMP rewards. This holds only when the improver's natural edit rate is p >= ~0.2. Below that rate, the Delta0 reward wins because of a predictable cost crossover.

**If opposite.** All meta-rewards give the same held-out Q^(1) within an equivalence margin of 0.05. ### Forced Meta-Edits in Long Loops: Do Quota and Gated Improver Revisions Stack at Matched Compute? (`GAP-3`, gap) — panel 63.3/100

**Claim.** Over 80 generations at equal dollars, a gated meta-edit quota raises Q^(1) at generation 80 by >= 0.05 over natural self-editing, while a placebo quota does not. The quota forces a diagnose-the-improver step every k=5 generations, and an edit is kept only if Q1-hat improves. The gain mostly arrives with the first routing edit and saturates after that, so meta-gains do not stack.

**If opposite.** Gains keep stacking, with Q^(1) rising monotonically through generation 80. Or the quota fails to beat the placebo, meaning the forcing only consumes budget. Either would overturn the saturation story.

**Design.** Arms: (1) natural self-editing, the IbI baseline with edit rates of 1-15%; (2) an ungated quota using IbI's positive-control diagnose prompt every k=5 generations; (3) a placebo quota whose 'edit' renders M0 unchanged but bills the same tokens; (4) two-timescale updates with an outer improver update every 10 generations, MetaSkill-Evolve-style; (5) a gated quota that adopts an edit only if Q1-hat (from MR-C) beats the parent. Each arm runs 4 seeds x 80 generations on Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B with total dollars matched, including meta-edit costs. To avoid IbI's saturation, where every seed hit a perfect dev score by generation 37, we use a procedurally refreshed bank with tunable difficulty. Q^(1) is scored by nested replay at generations 0/20/40/80 on 24 meta-states x 6 replicates, with p and mu reported. IbI's power result (6 meta-states resolve only large effects) motivates this size. Pre-registered tests: the arm-5 vs arm-1 contrast at generation 80 (TOST margin 0.05, hierarchical bootstrap), and the shape of the Q^(1) trajectory (piecewise fit, saturation vs linear). We also report the break-even horizon at which forced meta-edit cost is repaid in task score. Estimated full cost is about $900.

**Pilot.** One week, about $55. Run arms 1, 3 and 5, 1 seed x 25 generations each, scoring Q^(1) at generations 0 and 25 on 8 meta-states x 3 replicates. The go signal is that the gated quota adopts at least 2 improver edits and the refreshed bank keeps dev below 0.9.

### Affordable Successor Quality: Hurdle and Diff-Reading Estimators of Q^(1) and Their Sample-Complexity Law (`MR-C`, meta-reward) — panel 62.8/100

**Design.** We build a reference set of 40 improvers: M0, M1, IbI's released successors, the positive-control modifier, and improvers harvested from DGM, Hyperagents and Gödel Agent style loops run on Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B. Theory: a sample-complexity bound of order 1/(p·eps^2) for the hurdle estimator, a matching lower bound for score-only estimators, and an extension to order 2 at order (1/p)^2. The fitted law is validated by manipulating p with a diagnose-prompt dose, which IbI shows moves the edit rate from 11/42 to 35/42. Estimated full cost is about $600.

**Pilot.** One week, about $40. Use 10 improvers (M0-like and M1-like seeds plus 8 harvested ones), with full replay on 8 meta-states x 3 replicates as provisional ground truth. ### Order Reversal in the Wild: Routing Edits Raise Task Gain but Suppress Meta-Editing (`GAP-1`, gap) — panel 62.1/100

**Claim.** Among 40-60 real improver edits harvested from 5 self-improvement loop families, Delta0 and the change in edit propensity Delta_p are negatively correlated (corr < -0.3). At least 15% of Delta0-positive edits have Delta1 < 0, which is the first empirical case of IbI's Prop 1 order reversal, and it is driven by evidence-routing edits.

**If opposite.** corr(Delta0, Delta_p) >= 0 and order reversals occur below 5%. Order-0 and order-1 quality are then aligned in practice, and Prop 1 is a curiosity, which would support order-0 selection.

**Design.** We harvest improver edits from DGM, Hyperagents, Gödel Agent, HGM and RSI-Harness-style loops, re-run on Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B so that the backbone is controlled, plus IbI's released edits. That gives 40-60 edits, each a parent/child pair. For each pair we measure Delta0 by common-target replay, p and mu by order-1 nested replay on 24 meta-states x 6 replicates, and Delta1. This includes a re-analysis of IbI's own hidden signal, where M1's 4/36 edits of M0 averaged 0.86 against 0.57 for M0's own edits. Edits are classified as routing, budget, reword or structural. Pre-registered tests: corr(Delta0, Delta_p) < 0 (permutation test), the frequency of {Delta0 > 0, Delta1 < 0} with a Wilson interval, and a mediation analysis checking whether routing reduces p. We also follow 3 M0-started and 3 M1-started lineages for 40 generations and track p_t and Q^(1). A replication on Llama-3.3-70B uses 20 edits. Following IbI's simulation (power 0.51 at R=2 vs 0.93 at R=6), we use R=6 throughout and never fewer than 24 meta-states. Estimated full cost is about $500.

**Pilot.** One week, about $45. Take 12 edits (IbI's released ones plus 6 from a quick Hyperagents run on Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B) and measure Delta0, p and mu on 8 meta-states x 3 replicates. The go signal is enough variance in p across edits (range at least 0.2) for the correlation to be estimable.

**Delta.** Vs IbI: IbI proves order reversal can exist (Prop 1, a point-mass construction) and hints that routing lowers p, but it has no empirical case and its order-1 evidence rests on essentially one edit on one backbone. Vs Looped-RSI / RSI-Harness / MetaEvoForge / Meta-TTL: these select improvers on downstream or descendant gains and implicitly assume that order-0 and order-1 quality are aligned. This idea tests that assumption on their edit types.

### Harness Gains Are Mostly Routing: An Information-Redundancy Law Across Harness Evolvers and Model Strength (`GAP-2`, gap) — panel 59.6/100

**Claim.** Across 3 published harness-evolution methods and 8 open models of graded agentic strength, a single forced-read pointer recovers >= 60% of the evolved harness's gain. Gain follows (1-r)·u·(V_report - V_spec), where r is the model's unprompted read rate, and it falls to about zero for models with r > 0.8. Order-1 Delta1 for routing-saturated backbones is likewise about zero.

**If opposite.** Forced-read recovers less than 30% of the evolved gain and gain does not decline with r. Evolved harnesses then encode non-routing competence that persists for strong agentic models, contradicting the 'vanishes as models get more agentic' hypothesis.

**Design.** Models: Qwen3 at 4B/8B/32B/235B, Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B, Llama-3.3-70B, DeepSeek-V3.1 and GLM-4.6, all open-weight and cheap via API. For every (model, evolver) pair we measure evolved-harness gain, forced-read-graft gain, and unprompted read rate r. On 3 backbones (weakest reader, mid, strongest reader) we run order-1 nested replay of the evolved improver on 24 meta-states x 6 replicates; the 24 x 6 design follows IbI's power analysis. Following IbI's finding that DeepSeek gets 0.000 and Gemini -0.109 under forced reading, we pre-register a negative slope of gain on r. ### Openness x Meta-Reward: When Wider Editable Surfaces Help or Hurt Improver Evolution (`MR-D`, meta-reward) — panel 58.2/100

**Claim.** Meta-reward and editable-surface size interact, with crossing effects. Under child-score selection, widening the improver surface (instruction -> +tools -> +evidence summarizer -> +selection code -> +memory) increases the regression rate and leaves Delta1 at zero or below. Under Q1-hat selection, the same widening raises Delta1 by >= 0.05 at the widest surface, because the extra surface makes non-routing meta-edits possible.

**If opposite.** There is no interaction. Either surface size has no effect, because the improver only ever edits the instruction text, as most IbI-selected versions did, or the two rewards shift together. In that case the 'openness law' is irrelevant to meta-reward design.

**Design.** The 2 x 4 factorial uses the meta-reward (child score vs Q1-hat from MR-C) and the surface: S1 is the instruction text only, which is IbI's effective surface since the harness ignores the cap. S2 adds tool definitions and the budget. S3 adds an evidence-summarizer module and parent-selection code. S4 adds a persistent memory file. Each of the 8 cells runs 3 seeds x 50 generations on Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B at matched dollars, and the budget is held fixed by the harness so that gains cannot come from extra spend. The outcomes are p, mu, Delta0, held-out Delta1 (nested replay, 24 meta-states x 6 replicates), regression rate (the fraction of promoted improvers with Q^(1) below their parent's), and a diff-category histogram (routing, budget, reword, structural). The sample size follows IbI's power analysis: 6 meta-states detect only large effects, while R=6 gives 0.93 power for a large edit-rate shift. We therefore pool across 24 meta-states and test the interaction contrast with a hierarchical bootstrap and a pre-registered sign. One replication of the S1 and S4 cells uses Llama-3.3-70B. Estimated full cost is about $900.

**Pilot.** One week, about $55. Run 4 cells (S1 and S4 x both rewards), 1 seed x 15 generations each, scored on 8 meta-states x 3 replicates. The go signal is that S4 produces non-routing structural edits (memory or selection code) in at least 20% of improver edits, so the surface is actually used.

**Delta.** Vs IbI: IbI tests only a single-operator-file surface and never varies surface size. Released configurations with other surfaces are audited only for persistence. Vs Looped-RSI / RSI-Harness / MetaEvoForge / Meta-TTL: these fix one surface (a harness or a meta-agent prompt) and one reward. None treats surface openness as a factor or reports how it interacts with the meta-reward at order 1.

**Scoop check: medium-risk.** I ran about 16 searches (11 on ICLR 2027 OpenReview, 2 on alphaXiv, 3 on Semantic Scholar 2026; the Semantic Scholar keyword searches came back empty and I used it only to pull abstracts), and nothing tests the full design: a graded improver surface crossed with the meta-reward, measured on held-out Delta1. VERSE (2610.02616), however, already reports the qualitative crossing in binary form: a self-evolving optimizer fails without verification and is best with it. A reviewer will say "VERSE showed this." Reposition along four lines.

(1) Cite VERSE as the motivating 2x2 and claim what it lacks: a dose-response over S1 to S4, a true meta-reward manipulation (child score vs Q1-hat, used for selecting improvers rather than as a tool the optimizer can call), and order-1 outcomes (Delta1 via nested replay, a lineage regression rate, a diff-category histogram). None of those appear in VERSE.

(2) Add a third surface arm, or a control, that gives a pre-submission verification tool at S2. Then VERSE's 'verification' can be separated from the meta-reward. Without this, a reviewer can argue that Q1-hat simply acts as verification.

(3) Pre-register the falsifiable shape. Under child-score selection, regression rises monotonically with surface size and Delta1 stays at or below 0. Under Q1-hat, Delta1 at S4 exceeds Delta1 at S1 by at least 0.05. Report the location of the crossover, which no paper has.

(4) Use the diff histogram to make the mechanism claim: under Q1-hat the share of non-routing edits grows with the surface. This ties back to IbI's finding that its effective surface is only the instruction text, and VERSE does not address it.

Also cite HMED for replay-based meta-credit, and RRSI and Beneath the Surface for task-level scope limits and admission effects. The window is about 6 months before NeurIPS 2027, and harness-RSI papers are appearing weekly, so do a fast S1-vs-S4 pilot of both meta-rewards (4 cells) first.
