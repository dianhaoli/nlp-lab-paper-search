### At a glance
- **What this corner is.** It covers four kinds of loop:
  - **Co-evolution:** a harness (prompts, tools, memory, middleware, control logic) and model weights are improved in one loop, alternating or jointly.
  - **Distillation:** harness-induced behaviour is moved into weights so the harness can be removed.
  - **Improver weights:** the weights of the model that *writes* harnesses are trained (Evo²).
  - **Baldwin question:** does a supportive harness during training *help* the weights learn ("assist") or *substitute for* learning ("hiding", i.e. harness dependence)?
- **Size.** As a *method* lane it is saturated:
  - about 25 arXiv method papers between Jul and Nov 2026;
  - more than 12 ICLR 2027 co-evolution submissions (round-2 sweep †);
  - 9 more ICLR 2027 harness-distillation or internalization submissions found this session ◐.
- **State of the art, by setting:**
  - **Distillation, single round.** Harness-Zero: Qwen3.5-9B with the specialized harness removed scores 44.3 macro-average. That beats the base model *with* the evolved harness (41.7) ✓.
  - **Joint RL.** JoMoHa reaches 97.0% on ALFWorld ✓.
  - **Multi-round.** CoEvo-RSI runs 10 rounds × 3 runs ✓.
  - **Long-horizon continual learning.** HERD runs 25 stages × 3 seeds and deploys bare weights ✓.
- **Consensus 1: one-sided optimisation leaves gains on the table.** Every co-evolution paper beats its harness-only and weight-only arms (SIA, WHALE, JoMoHa, CoEvo-RSI, Self-HPO, HarnessAdapt-RL ✓). Caveat: no paper matches compute across arms.
- **Consensus 2: naive coupling breaks.**
  - Alternating updates can collapse: JoMoHa 90.5 → 31.5 ✓.
  - Co-evolved pairs become mutually specialised (CODA ✓, CoEvo-RSI "brain–body mismatch" ✓).
  - Plain SFT on harness-supported trajectories creates harness dependence (Harness Annealing ✓, TAME ◐, harness-entanglement l8aeyLANkN ◐).
- **Emerging regularity, the Baldwin sign.** Support *assists* weight learning when it is gated to all-fail cases, expressed in the deployment action space, and supervises decisions rather than answers. It *hides* learning when it is always on or answer-revealing. A shared mechanism is proposed: the share of GRPO groups with mixed outcomes. Evidence: HarnessAdapt-RL, Harness-S, Teacher-free co-evolution, Harness-Zero Table 3 ✓.
- **Biggest open problem.** No compute-matched, multi-seed, per-round decomposition (harness effect × weight effect × interaction, with a placebo) exists for any multi-round loop. We therefore do not know whether harness↔weights loops *compound* or simply front-load one round of gains. A second gap: no work trains the improver on a successor-quality signal; TAME's post-update transfer reward is the closest ◐.

### Taxonomy
The surveys split this corner on two axes. "What is updated" (θ = weights, Σ = scaffold, i.e. the harness) comes from [2607.13104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13104) §3.2 ✓. "Distributional vs action-level self-reference" comes from the same section. Ren et al. list "Parametric Distillation and Joint Optimization" as an open direction because of its credit-assignment problem: should a failure be fixed by a prompt, a tool wrapper or a gradient? ✓ (§10, item 3). The alphaXiv survey [2611.recursive-self-improvement-llm-agents](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.recursive-self-improvement-llm-agents) explicitly *excludes* executor-weight updates, so this corner sits outside its scope ✓. Duan et al. [2609.11873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11873) supply the needed test: "structural vs effective recursion". Does a revised mechanism persist, *and* does it yield better successors under matched budgets ✓ (§1.7)? No paper in this chapter meets the "effective" bar.

### Paper cards

**Harness-Zero: Harness Distillation via Agent-as-Harness** ([2609.24974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24974), 21 Sep 2026, ICLR27 sub rZ7eO7DZmc; PKU/Google/HKUST)
- *What it does.* A harnessing agent reads a reference harness K (adapted from the evolved harness h⋆) and reviews each student proposal before it is executed. It passes the proposal or replaces it with a minimal correction expressed in the *target* harness's action space. The student is then SFT'd on these reviewed trajectories.
- *Setup.*
  - Student: Qwen3.5-9B. Harnessing agent: GPT-5.6 Sol. h⋆ evolved by Kimi K3.
  - Target harness: mini-SWE-agent with a single Bash tool.
  - Benchmarks: SpreadsheetBench Verified (100 test), AppWorld (168 test), USPTO (100 test).
  - Training: LoRA rank 32, 2 epochs, 487/282/500 rollouts, **seed 42 only**, single-run pass@1 (§4.1, App. E.3 ✓).
- *Key results.*
  - Macro-average under h rises from 23.3 to 44.3, versus 41.7 for the base model with h⋆ attached ✓ (Table 2).
  - USPTO: distilled 30.0 vs 38.0 with h⋆. Domain knowledge transfers less well than procedure ✓ (Obs. 3).
  - Ablation on USPTO only: SFT on teacher-under-h⋆ trajectories drops to 3.0 (action-space mismatch). Oracle-answer review reaches 98.6% collection success but only 15.0 test. K-guided review reaches 30.0 ✓ (Table 3).
  - 82.3% recovery across 28 harness-exclusive behaviours ✓ (Table 4).
  - At inference, agent-as-harness (81.1) beats code-as-harness (78.1) on frontier models ✓ (Table 1). The advantage turns negative on weaker reviewers: −12.0 on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B ✓ (Table 8).
- *Limitations and critiques.*
  - One seed; ablation on one domain.
  - Review costs 2.4× latency on USPTO.
  - Our ICLR sweep notes it never compares against a frozen-harness RL baseline †.
  - It is the purest instance of "amplify-then-distill", and it shows naive distillation of amplified trajectories *fails*. It works only when supervision is re-expressed in the deployment action space.

**Harness Evolution Hits a Ceiling: When Weight Training Should Begin** ([2610.11655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11655), 8 Oct 2026, ICLR27 sub FPaMYO47j4)
- *What it does.* Runs a self-evolving harness loop on DeepPlanning, then crosses seed vs evolved harness × base vs LoRA weights. Failures are labelled F1–F4: process (blocked calls, loops, exhausted budgets) vs content (a poor delivered plan).
- *Setup.*
  - Qwen3.5-4B and 9B, plus 7 other lines.
  - 48 dev and 72 held-out tasks; 4 rollouts per cell; same-night anchors.
  - Transfer check: WebArena-Lite, 117 unseen tasks.
- *Key results.*
  - Held-out score: 4B 0.164 → 0.297, 9B 0.323 → 0.442 ✓ (Table 1).
  - LoRA on evolved-harness trajectories, under the *seed* harness: +0.126 (4B) and +0.131 (9B) held-out ✓.
  - On 4B the adapter stacks with the harness (+0.082). On 9B it substitutes (+0.007) ✓.
  - The answer-shuffled placebo falls 0.158 (dev) and 0.227 (held-out) below base, **9B only** ✓.
  - Full fine-tuning adds +0.044 / +0.042 ✓.
  - The 9B adapter cuts F4 (content failures) from 28% to 5% held-out ✓.
  - WebArena-Lite: harness gain +0.088. Adapters add nothing because the gain lives in "what the model sees" ✓ (§7).
- *Limitations and critiques.*
  - 2-rollout ablation cells; no matched-budget test-time-search baseline; a converter inflates scores by about 0.025 ✓.
  - Only one harness→weights round.
  - The authors say the ceiling belongs to the loop's evidence package, not to harness evolution in general ✓ (§6.2).

**CoEvo-RSI: When Harnesses Evolve, Which Models Should Follow?** ([2610.25078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25078), 18 Oct 2026, ICLR27 sub 7UXKgr1Akp; NUS/A*STAR)
- *What it does.* A meta-agent draws on a shared archive of harnesses, checkpoints and histories. Each round it develops a harness, selects or merges a checkpoint, and adapts the model by SFT on its own rejection-sampled trajectories. It introduces the Turn-level Interface Mismatch Rate (TIMR).
- *Setup.*
  - Qwen3.5-9B; meta-agent DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash in Codex.
  - 10 rounds, K=8 candidates; full-parameter SFT on 4×H100; averaged over 3 runs.
  - Tasks: Who&When, TraceElephant, RBAC Spider/BIRD, HomeBench ✓ (§4.1).
- *Key results.*
  - TraceElephant Who accuracy 70.78 vs 56.62 for DeepSeek-V4-Pro ✓ (Table 1).
  - Who&When Algo: 62.17, *below* direct DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at 68.25 ✓.
  - Ablations on Who&When Algo, all single-benchmark ✓ (§4.3):
    - Harness-only has TIMR > 1% in 8/10 rounds;
    - Model-only reaches at most 35% SA;
    - Latest-only regresses at round 4 and ends lowest;
    - the full method reuses **round-4 weights at rounds 6 and 8**.
  - *Correction:* our round-1 note said "round-3 weights at round 10".
- *Limitations.* No compute-matched decomposition. Same-domain held-out only. TIMR captures interface violations only (authors' caveat ✓).

**HERD: Harness-Evolving Recursive Distillation for Continual Learning** ([2610.31401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31401), 22 Oct 2026; not found on ICLR27 †)
- *What it does.* At every stage of a task stream: the harness evolves skills plus a bounded high-residual memory; the frozen previous checkpoint, given privileged context (skill + verified answer), teaches the student; distillation is drift-constrained by two anchors (previous checkpoint, base θ0). Only the weights are deployed.
- *Setup.* Qwen3.5-4B; TRACE+17 with 25 stages; 3 seeds; single-seed ablations ✓.
- *Key results* (Table 1 ✓):
  - AA 0.706. Acquisition 0.713 vs 0.715 for Seq-LoRA (statistically tied).
  - Forgetting 0.010 vs 0.019–0.032 for the parametric baselines. ΔC −0.063 vs −0.20 to −0.24.
  - Context-only learners (ACE 0.427, HCL 0.433) barely beat base (0.419). The harness alone does almost nothing on this stream.
  - Without skills: acquisition −4.5 and AA −5.0 ✓ (Fig. 4). The harness's job is to choose *what* to distill.
  - Attaching the harness after distillation changes metrics by ≤0.3 ✓.
  - Cost: 10,723 s total vs 10,463 s for Seq-LoRA; OPHSD needs 659,000 s ✓.
- *Limitations.* One model, one stream, known task boundaries, verified answers required ✓. The skill writer is fixed at θ0, so the writer does not co-evolve †.

**JoMoHa: Joint Model–Harness Optimization with RL** ([2611.11499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11499), 8 Nov 2026; HKU/Salesforce)
- *What it does.* Trains an Engineer (writes executable harness code) and a Solver jointly by RL. They share the task reward, with separate critics, plus peer weighting, schedule and value normalisation.
- *Setup.* Qwen3.5-9B-class actors; 144 rounds × 8 paired episodes; ALFWorld, WebShop, DBBench. Baselines mostly copied from Harness-R1 ✓ (§6).
- *Key results.*
  - Naive alternation on ALFWorld: 83.0 → 90.5 → 78.5 → 48.5 → 31.5 ✓ (Table 2).
  - Fixed-harness cross-evaluation decomposes the decline: −47.5 = −34.5 (Solver decline, measured with the harness removed) + −13.0 (compatibility loss) ✓ (Eq. 2).
  - Joint training reaches 97.0, vs 90.5 for Frozen Engineer, 89.5 weight-only and 80.5 harness-only ✓.
  - 3-environment average 70.3 vs 63.1 for WHALE (locally run) ✓ (Table 4).
- *Limitations.*
  - Single runs; no seeds; no compute matching against RL alone.
  - The collapse diagnosis comes from one alternating run.
  - The decomposition shows co-adaptation damages the *standalone Solver*, which is Baldwin hiding inside co-evolution.

**Harness Annealing: Learning to Act with Less External Control** ([2610.01235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01235), 1 Oct 2026, ICLR27 sub HOk367J7I0; SJTU/Berkeley)
- *What it does.* Defines "harness internalization": learning to take over specified control responsibilities once runtime support is withdrawn. The nested harnesses are H1 tools ⊂ H2 +state ⊂ H3 +workflow ⊂ H4 +verification. HAT combines decision-level ("Layered") supervision with a curriculum over teacher trajectories from progressively weaker harnesses.
- *Setup.* Qwen3.5-9B and 35B; SWE-QA and SWE-QA-Pro; scores from a GPT-5.5 reference-based judge; LoRA rank 64; **single seed** ✓ (App. A).
- *Key results.*
  - Direct SFT on H4 trajectories scores **16.51 under H1, vs 59.93 for the untrained base** ✓ (Table 4). Even Direct H1-trained reaches only 32.45.
  - Layered H4-trained reaches 71.99 under H1. The H2-targeted checkpoint reaches 77.00 ✓ (Table 3).
  - At 35B, gains are smaller (base 78.14 under H1) ✓.
- *Limitations.* No equal-budget controls. Direct and Layered are not matched for tokens. The judge-based metric is a caveat. Our round-2 sweep calls this the strongest owner of "harness dependence plus a fix" †.

**Evolving Together, Failing Apart: The Skill–Policy Co-evolution Trap (CODA)** ([2610.20845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20845), 14 Oct 2026, ICLR27 sub ZIBdUe2hGK; Alibaba)
- *What it does.* Argues that "cooperative acceptance" (accept a bank edit if it helps the *current* policy) ties the policy to its bank. It audits 4 co-evolution methods under semantic rewrites (T1), incomplete skills (T2) and misleading skills (T3).
- *Setup.* SkillRL, ReSkill, Evolving-RL, ARISE; 6 method–benchmark settings.
- *Key results.*
  - Worst-case success is 4.9–12.6 points below peak, erasing 52–93% of the skill gain ✓ (Table 2).
  - The *meaning-preserving* rewrite is the worst case in 3/6 settings. Consistency 77.5–87.0 vs a noise floor of 91.9–94.4 ✓.
  - In one setting, the co-evolved policy without its bank scores 57.6% vs 70.6% for a policy never trained with skills ✓ (App.). The pure-RL run there is itself unstable: peak 87.5, then 53.1; n=5 checkpoints ✓.
- *Limitations.* Diagnoses without fixing. Skills only, not harness code or model upgrades. The bands reflect evaluation noise, not training seeds ✓.

**Evo²: Evolving the Evolver through Weight–Harness Co-Evolution** ([2610.13025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13025), 9 Oct 2026, ICLR27 sub Ppb1YoSRol; Google DeepMind/UIUC)
- *What it does.* One policy plays Proposer (writes harness edits) and Reflector (diagnoses edits) over a tree search with crossover. EvoTIDE trains *the evolver* with SFT plus interleaved GRPO on the outcomes of harness edits. **The task executor stays frozen** ✓ (§3.1). So this is improver-weights × harness co-evolution, not executor co-evolution.
- *Setup.* Qwen3.8-27B (FP8, greedy) as executor and evolver; Polyglot, SWE-bench Verified, LiveCodeBench; memory tasks LoCoMo and LongMemEval.
- *Key results.*
  - Polyglot 82.1 vs 62.6 for GEA; SWE-V 48.3 vs 41.0 for HGM; LCB 58.0 vs 56.0 ✓ (Table 1).
  - Isolating weight evolution: Polyglot +16.3 (p=.001), SWE-V +5.0 (p=.086), LoCoMo +9.3 ✓ (Table 5).
  - Best transfer in 5/6 directions ✓ (Table 2).
- *Limitations and critiques.*
  - In the proprietary-model table, Evo² reports "the better of its two top-ranked harnesses" while baselines report one, a selection asymmetry ✓ (§4.4).
  - The SWE-V gain is not significant.
  - The reward is order-0 (child gain), which is relevant to Dan's idea #5.

**SIA: Self Improving AI with Harness & Weight Updates** ([2605.27276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27276), May 2026; Hexo Labs)
- *What it does.* A Feedback-Agent iterates the scaffold, then switches to LoRA weight updates once harness progress stalls.
- *Setup.* gpt-oss-120b, LoRA rank 32; LawBench (191-class), AlphaEvolve TriMul, MAGIC denoising.
- *Key results* (Table 3 ✓):
  - LawBench: 13.5 → 50.0 (harness only) → 70.1 (harness + weights).
  - TriMul: 0.105 → 0.120 → 1.475.
  - Denoising: 0.048 → 0.241 → 0.289.
- *Limitations.* Sequential, not interleaved. No weight-only arm, no seeds; TriMul is test-time with no split. §8 names "coupled co-evolutionary Goodhart", meaning two optimisers against one verifier, but does not measure it ✓.

**WHALE: A Simple Recipe for Joint Harness–Weight Optimization** ([2609.00196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00196), 31 Aug 2026, ICLR27 sub Gu5Zol8WcQ; KRAFTON/KAIST/Stanford, with Finn)
- *What it does.* Alternates online rejection-sampling fine-tuning with Meta-Harness search, using fixed or patience-based switching.
- *Setup.* Qwen3.5-2B/4B; SearchQA (7 sets), AIME24/25, Lichess puzzles; metric mean@8.
- *Key results* (✓ Tables 2–3):
  - SearchQA: 48.34 vs weight-only 38.27 and harness-only 38.29.
  - Math: 24.79 vs 15.42 and 0.42. Harness search helps math only after weights move.
  - Stagewise: 43.02 SearchQA. Adaptive WHALE: 52.82 with fewer rollouts.
- *Limitations.* Reports **"best test mean@8"**, i.e. best point along the test curve ✓. No seeds reported in the parts read. JoMoHa's local re-run gets 80.5 on ALFWorld for WHALE ✓.

**HarnessAdapt-RL: Training the Whole Agent, Not Just the Model** ([2610.28069](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28069), 20 Oct 2026; ZJU)
- *What it does.* During GRPO, a frozen teacher writes task-specific "mechanism-level" skills (removed at evaluation). A second teacher evolves tools and hooks (kept), accepting a change if the pass or trainable rate improves without lowering the other.
- *Setup.* A 9B base; SWE-bench Verified, Multilingual and Pro; 3 evaluation repeats of **one training run per configuration** ✓.
- *Key results* (Table 1 ✓):
  - Average 44.9 vs 35.3 for GRPO and 39.0 for OpenHands-GRPO.
  - Near-answer hints during training, evaluated after removal: **32.7 < 35.3 for GRPO**. Mechanism skills: 38.3.
  - Mixed-outcome group share: 46.5% (mechanism) vs 39.0% (hint) ✓ (App.).
  - Re-evolving tools as the policy changes beats freezing early versions (+2.2/+4.6/+2.0) ✓.
  - Removing the evolved tools at deployment costs up to 7.7 points ◐.
- *Limitations.* SWE only, one model, no training seeds. The acceptance rule is not a significance test ✓.

**Harness-S: Learning from Sibling Harnesses** ([2611.04277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04277), 3 Nov 2026; SJTU/Xiaohongshu)
- *What it does.* Builds a fixed pool of sibling harnesses. These are invoked *only when all base-harness rollouts fail*; the same policy generates the rescue rollouts, which replace the failed GRPO group. The policy is evaluated back under H0.
- *Setup.* Qwen3.5-4B/9B; AppWorld, SWE-Gym, DBBench; 3 randomized runs × 3 rollouts ✓ (Table 1).
- *Key results.*
  - 4B AppWorld Avg@3 under H0: 56.87 ±1.47 vs GRPO 48.98, H0-rescue 51.91, always-on mixtures 46.69/47.84 ✓.
  - 9B: 62.34 vs 53.31 ✓.
  - Effective-group rate 79.38% vs 64.69% for GRPO ✓.
- *Limitations.* The sibling pool is fixed and built by a frontier model. "Independent runs" may be evaluation randomisation rather than training seeds; unclear in the parts read.

**Self-HPO: Self-Evolving Coding Agents via Harness–Policy Optimization** ([2610.22791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22791), 16 Oct 2026, ICLR27 sub LxC7C6AMxv; MSR)
- *What it does.* The policy analyses its own failures and proposes harnesses (H-step), then is trained by RL under the chosen harness (P-step). Hard tasks seed the next search.
- *Setup.* Qwen3.5-35B-A3B; mini-SWE-agent seed harness; SWE-bench Verified plus OOD sets.
- *Key results.*
  - Cross-evaluation 2×2 on SWE-V (Table 3 ✓): P0/H0 61.0; P0/H2 64.5; P2/H0 64.4; P2/H2 69.5.
  - The joint gain (8.5) exceeds the sum of the individual gains (6.9) by 1.6, a **superadditive** interaction.
  - Average resolve rate 57.8 → 64.8 ◐.
- *Limitations.* One model family. The ± values look like evaluation spread, not training seeds. One 2×2 at the end of the run, not per round.

**Knowledge in the Harness, Reliability in the Weights: Teacher-Free Co-Evolution** ([2611.15268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15268), 11 Nov 2026; Brightendata)
- *What it does.* A Gemma 4 12B agent's own base model diagnoses failed τ²-bench conversations and writes rules into skills. Rules pass code checks, counterexample self-review, a paired retest and a noise-calibrated gate. GRPO then trains a LoRA under the new rules.
- *Key results.*
  - After 2 rounds: +12.5 pass^1 (retail) and +19.5 (airline) ✓ (abstract in PDF).
  - The 2×2 shows rules carry policy *knowledge* that RL with rules in context does not internalize, while weights make *already-solved* tasks reliable ✓.
  - Rules raise RL signal: in airline round 1, never-solved batch tasks fell from 71 to 42 ✓ (§5).
- *Limitations.* One model, two domains, 2 rounds; 114/50 official tasks, so single updates are mostly not significant ✓. A DeepSeek simulator is used instead of the leaderboard's.

**Co-Harness (Meituan/AI2)** ([2607.22688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22688), 17 Jul 2026, ICLR27 sub 53Ak0jvesg)
- *What it does.* A HarnessCritic attributes failures and proposes validated local diffs; SFT on the improved-harness trajectories follows.
- *Key results.* Qwen3-8B/32B on AIME24/25 and HMMT25: average 58.5 (R0) → 73.5 (R1) → 78.9 (R2) ✓ (Table 6).
- *Critique.* 2 rounds. No SFT-under-fixed-harness control, so the weight effect is not separated. Tiny test sets. The 22-version case study runs on AIME24 itself ✓.

**HELIX** ([2608.13951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13951), 14 Aug 2026; HKU)
- *What it does.* A source-traceable harness substrate (96 ports; 4,096 recipes) for "build–update–rebuild".
- *Key results.* +4.0% coverage over Pi on 100 AtCoder public-test tasks. Exports 438 verified training records ✓.
- *Critique.* **No model update is run.** "Comparing held with rebuilt harnesses" is listed as future work ✓ (§9–10).

**WeAct: Dynamic Weight Switching as Agent Actions** ([2610.26847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26847), Oct 2026; CMU/Duke, Yin co-first author)
- *What it does.* Capability LoRAs (plan/search/write) over frozen Qwen3-32B. Loading and offloading an adapter are actions in the tool-calling interface.
- *Key results.* WeAct-hard averages 45.47 vs 42.80 for Full SFT and 35.15–35.66 for merging baselines across 8 deep-research benchmarks ✓ (Table 1). One code-adapter insertion and one writing refresh.
- *Critique.* Hand-defined split. "Sustained continual learning … open" ✓ (Limitations).
- *Relevance.* It turns weights into a harness-like, editable library, a natural target for harness-style evolution.

**SEAL: Self-Adapting Language Models** ([2506.10943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10943), Jun 2025)
- *What it does.* RL on self-generated fine-tuning data ("self-edits"), rewarded by post-update accuracy.
- *Key results.*
  - Knowledge incorporation (Qwen2.5-7B): no-context SQuAD 33.5 → 47.0, vs 46.3 for GPT-4.1 synthetic data ✓.
  - ARC few-shot on Llama-3.2-1B: 72.5% vs 20% (self-edits without RL) and 0% (ICL) ✓.
- *Critique.* Catastrophic forgetting under sequential self-edits. Expensive TTT reward loop ✓ (§Limitations).

### Consensus, debates & contradictions
- **Consensus: both levers are needed, but each does different work.** The papers converge on a division of labour:
  - harness for process, knowledge and "what the model sees" (Ceiling, Teacher-free, Not All Experience †);
  - weights for content quality and consistency (Ceiling's 9B F4 cut, Teacher-free "reliability in the weights", SIA "domain intuition").
- **Contradiction: harness-only value varies enormously by task.**
  - Near-useless: HERD's context-only learners (0.43 vs 0.70 for weights); WHALE math (0.42); SIA TriMul (0.120 vs 1.475).
  - Large: Harness-Zero's h⋆ (+18.4); Ceiling 4B (+0.13).
  - No paper predicts this *before* running except Ceiling's failure-composition rule (one benchmark).
- **Debate: is distillation better than the amplifier?**
  - Yes in procedural domains: Harness-Zero distilled 44.3 > h⋆ 41.7; Ceiling's 9B adapter alone matches the evolution line.
  - No for knowledge: USPTO 30 < 38; Ceiling's WebArena-Lite adapters add nothing.
  - How the data is built matters more than how good the teacher is. SFT on stronger teacher-under-h⋆ trajectories *hurts* (3.0). Oracle review (98.6% collection success) gives 15.0 test.
- **Debate: Baldwin assist vs hiding.** The same type of intervention has opposite signs across papers:
  - *Hiding:* HAT Direct SFT (16.51 vs 59.93 base); HarnessAdapt near-answer hints (32.7 < 35.3); always-on mixtures in Harness-S (46.69 < 48.98); CODA (57.6 vs 70.6 without the bank); JoMoHa (Solver −34.5 with no harness); TAME rank reversal ◐; harness entanglement −9.8 ◐.
  - *Assist:* gated rescue (Harness-S +7.9); mechanism skills (+3.0); AutoScaffold (+7.2 ◐); Layered HAT; Self-HPO P2/H0 (+3.4); Ceiling LoRA under the seed harness (+0.13); Teacher-free rules shrinking never-solved tasks 71 → 42.
  - Proposed reconciliation: support helps when it *creates* within-group reward contrast on all-fail tasks and is expressed in actions the bare policy can take; it hurts when it makes success trivial or acts outside the policy's action space. This is supported by mixed-group statistics in three papers, but **no paper manipulates support dose while holding everything else fixed**.
- **Contradiction: does co-evolution compound?**
  - Co-Harness claims "genuine compounding" over 2 rounds with no control.
  - JoMoHa shows alternation collapses after round 1.
  - CoEvo-RSI's best checkpoints are *old* weights re-selected.
  - WHALE shows small interleaving beats stagewise.
  - Net: gains concentrate in early rounds, and inheritance or selection does the stabilising.
- **Superadditivity vs substitution.** Self-HPO finds harness × weights superadditive (+1.6). Ceiling finds stacking on 4B but substitution on 9B. Interaction sign likely depends on model strength; untested systematically.
- **Who writes the harness.** External frontier writers dominate (WHALE, CoEvo-RSI, Harness-Zero, HarnessAdapt). Same-model writers work with heavy gating (Teacher-free, Self-HPO, HASE). Training the writer (JoMoHa +6.5 over Frozen Engineer; Evo² +16.3 Polyglot) helps, but the reward is order-0 (task score).

### Open problems
1. **Per-round, compute-matched decomposition of multi-round loops.** For each round t, measure SR(θ_t, H_t), SR(θ_t, H_0), SR(θ_0, H_t), SR(θ_0, H_0), plus an answer-shuffled placebo and seeds ≥ 3, under equal rollout and GPU budgets against weight-only RL and test-time search. JoMoHa does this once on one run; Self-HPO once at the end; Ceiling for one round.
2. **A dose-response for the Baldwin effect.** Vary support strength and adaptivity during GRPO (none / gated rescue / always-on hints / self-editing harness) with everything else fixed. Measure "retained after removal" and the mixed-group share as a mediator. TAME and HAT own the hiding diagnostic, so only the dose-response and mechanism are open.
3. **A prospective "which lever" predictor.** Ceiling's rule (F1–F4) and the routing rule in Not All Experience are post hoc on 1–2 benchmarks. A pre-registered predictor of harness-gain vs weight-gain across ≥ 5 domains is missing.
4. **Writer improves as weights internalize.** HERD's writer is θ0; Evo² trains the writer but freezes the executor; JoMoHa trains both but rewards only shared task success. Nobody measures order-1 improvement: whether the co-evolved writer writes better harnesses for a *fresh* executor.
5. **Robustness of the delivered pair.** Drift of the harness (CODA), of the model (upgrade), and of the deployment harness (entanglement, Policy-Is-a-Harness). CODA-style audits should become standard; no method is trained for drift-robustness except ARCHER (harness rendering) ◐ and TAME (reference harnesses) ◐.
6. **What is lost in distillation.** Ren et al. warn that consolidation discards rare error-recovery strategies ✓ (§10). Harness-Zero's per-pattern recovery is 40–100%. Whether safeguards and redundant checks survive is unmeasured (Masked Erosion lattice † + distillation).
7. **Selection hygiene.** "Best test mean@8" (WHALE), best-of-two (Evo²), test-split case studies (Co-Harness), and acceptance rules that are not significance tests (HarnessAdapt) are all common. Teacher-free shows that paired seeds do not pair dialogue outcomes (within-task correlation −0.09 to 0.07 ✓).
8. **Harness as weights.** WeAct adapters, Differentiable Harness caches and Latent Harness activation shifts are "harness-like" parameters. Can harness-evolution machinery (gates, archives, crossover) operate on adapter libraries, and does it avoid co-evolution lock-in?

### Where it is heading (to mid-2027)
- **Method papers** will keep appearing, but reviewers will demand the controls listed in Open problems 1 and 7. Expect ICLR 2027 reviews of WHALE, CoEvo-RSI and Co-Harness to cite JoMoHa's collapse and CODA's fragility as required baselines (forecast).
- **The internalization lane will standardise on metrics.** "Retained after support removal" (TAME/HAT) and per-behaviour recovery (Harness-Zero Table 4) will become the norm. On-policy self-distillation with privileged harness context (HERD, SkillDuet, MDSKILL, ENGRAM) will be the default transfer tool.
- **Training the harness writer by RL** will become mainstream (JoMoHa, Evo², TAME, ACE-RL, MECA). Debate will move to *what to reward*: task score, post-update transfer (TAME) or successor quality.
- **Gated rather than always-on support** in RL (Harness-S, Teacher-free, VACE) will become the default recipe. Baldwin-style analysis will be absorbed as "enrichment" theory (Understanding Enrichment 2610.02846 †).
- **Big-lab entry** (GDM Evo², MSR Self-HPO, Google 2611.22299, Meta PRD) suggests production RL pipelines will co-train harness and weights. Academic leverage shifts to measurement, theory and robustness audits.

### Connection to our ideas
- **#5 Order-0 Rewards Breed Routers.** Directly in this corner. Evo², MetaEvolve, JoMoHa's Engineer and ACE-RL all train improver weights on order-0 or child-gain rewards. TAME's post-update-transfer reward ◐ is the closest thing to a successor-quality reward and must be cited and differentiated. Evo²'s Table 5 (+16.3 / +5.0 n.s.) is a natural replication target for "does the trained evolver improve at order 1 on a fresh executor?"
- **#6 Meta-Overfitting.** Evo² claims evolved harnesses transfer in 5/6 directions, but these are all coding tasks with an evolver trained on them. CoEvo-RSI admits same-domain held-out only. A far-domain test of Evo²-style trained evolvers is a clean, cheap application of #6.
- **#1 Taught, Not Selected.** Distillation propagates whatever rules the harness learned. HERD, Harness-Zero and Teacher-free all write rules from traces, then train weights on them. A misleading-trace channel would become *parametric* after one distillation round. Teacher-free's counterexample self-review and gate are the obvious defense to compare against.
- **#7 Varying Goals / #9 MetaShift.** CODA (bank drift), Policy-Is-a-Harness (interface change) and CoEvo-RSI (old weights re-selected) give motivation. In a co-evolution loop, a "regime change" can come from the weights changing under a fixed harness, which is JoMoHa's −13.0 compatibility loss.
- **#2/#3 Forced Quota and Gate.** VACE (17/44 harmful proposals rejected ◐), CoEvo-RSI's meta-agent selection, Teacher-free's noise-calibrated gate and HarnessAdapt's non-significance acceptance are concrete gate types to include in a gate taxonomy.
- **Parked: Baldwin hiding/assist (#18) and harness→weights compounding.** Confirmed crowded. TAME, HAT, HarnessAdapt-RL, Harness-S and AutoScaffold together cover both signs. The only defensible remaining slice is the controlled dose-response with mixed-group mediation (Open problem 2). Multi-round compounding survives only as the measurement paper in Open problem 1.
- **Mentor link (WeAct).** WeAct is the natural "harness made of weights": adapter insertion and refresh as agent actions. A gated, archive-based evolution of the adapter library, borrowing CoEvo-RSI's checkpoint re-selection and CODA's drift audit, connects Dan's improver-side ideas to Xunjian's own line without entering the saturated co-evolution method lane.
