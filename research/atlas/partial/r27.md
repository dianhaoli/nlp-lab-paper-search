### At a glance
- **What this corner is.** Loops in which the system writes its own training material and learns from it. That material can be:
  - problems: proposer/challenger → solver;
  - opponents: zero-sum games;
  - whole executable environments: environment designer → agent;
  - or the order in which real tasks are seen: an adaptive curriculum over a pool.
- **How the surveys place it:**
  - Duan et al. (2609.11873) put it at **L3, "experience autonomy"**. The defining property is "learner-conditioned future experience acquisition". They ask that a task be *valid*, *at the right difficulty* and *durably beneficial*, and note that "neither correctness nor estimated difficulty alone establishes learning value" (§3.4, §6 ✓).
  - Zong et al. (2608.10299) file it under **Stage 1, agent–agent** (adversarial) and **Stage 2, agent–environment** (task, feedback and interaction-space co-evolution) ✓.
- **State of the art, weight learners (verifiable domains).** Zero-data loops add about +3 to +10 avg points on Qwen3-4B/8B-Base math/general suites:
  - AZR, R-Zero, SPICE, Agent0 ✓.
  - EvoHeir (2610.22029) adds an inheritance gate that keeps gains climbing for 10 rounds ✓.
  - SPADE moves from QA to **self-written Gym environments**: +8.1 over base and +5.3 over the best fixed-environment baseline at 30B ✓.
- **State of the art, frozen-weight (harness/memory) learners.** ActiveSaddler gets +4.4 on GAIA2 and +7.5 on TB2 over a fixed scenario order ✓. HERA and DAEDALUS show self-generated or co-evolved tasks can stand in for curated ones ◐.
- **Consensus.**
  1. Pure closed loops plateau and then collapse within about 3–10 rounds. Causes include pseudo-label decay, invalid questions, and diversity collapse with length inflation.
  2. **External grounding** postpones this:
     - a corpus (SPICE);
     - a few human anchors (R-Few with 1–5%, Anchored Self-Play with ≥50 references);
     - an executor (AZR);
     - a population or cross-fitting (PopuLoRA, CrossFit).
- **The 2026-H2 turn is audits, not methods.** Four papers:
  - "What Actually Evolves" (ICLR'27 submission) finds gains are mostly *sharpening* of items the model already solves, and that later proposers teach no more than earlier ones ◐.
  - Selection Is Not Learning finds 60 of 96 updates on verified tasks yield no gain ✓.
  - 2611.06965 finds that executor "grounding" bakes the generator's own false belief into the answer key ✓.
  - The placebo trial finds that, in code→math, correctness of the reward does matter; format alone does not explain AZR's gain ✓.
- **Biggest open problem.** We cannot yet show that a self-generated curriculum **teaches what the learner cannot already do**, measured against out-of-loop ground truth at matched compute. Related unknowns: how much external grounding is enough (the "exchange rate", 2607.07663 §5 ✓), and whether training the proposer is worth anything over a strong fixed generator plus selection.

### Taxonomy

The surveys split this corner along three axes:
- **What is generated:** Zong's task-space vs interaction-space.
- **Where the signal is grounded:** Chen's "data gating vs reward grounding"; Yu et al.'s verifiable vs unverifiable.
- **What learns:** Ren's distributional (θ) vs action-level (Σ) self-reference.

Duan's L3 criterion cuts across all three. Selection alone counts as L3 if it adapts to the learner across rounds; one-shot synthetic data does not.

### Paper cards

**Absolute Zero: Reinforced Self-play Reasoning with Zero Data** ([2505.03335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03335), May 2025, v3 Oct 2025; arXiv)
- *What it does.* A single model proposes deduction, abduction and induction Python tasks and solves them. A Python executor both validates tasks and checks answers.
- *Setup.*
  - Models: Qwen2.5-7B base, Coder, Instruct and Math; Coder-3B and 14B; Llama-3.1-8B.
  - Benchmarks: 3 code (HumanEval+, MBPP+, LCB) and 6 math. Greedy decoding.
  - Proposer reward: 1 − r̄_solve over G rollouts, with 0 for unsolvable or trivial tasks.
- *Key results.*
  - Qwen2.5-7B-Coder + AZR: overall 50.4 (+10.2), math +15.2, code +5.0 ✓ (§4.2).
  - Gains grow with scale: +5.7 / +10.2 / +13.2 for 3B / 7B / 14B Coder ✓.
  - Llama-3.1-8B: AZR 19.2 vs SimpleRL (human data) 20.5 ✓.
  - **Ablation (Table 2):** training the solver only, with an untrained prompted proposer, scores 45.4 vs 46.8 for full AZR. Proposer training is worth only +1.4 ✓. Dropping K-reference conditioning costs about 5 math points ✓.
  - Reports an "uh-oh moment" (unsafe chain of thought) on Llama ✓.
- *Limitations / critiques.*
  - Its own ablation shows a small proposer effect.
  - The placebo trial confirms released-checkpoint gains under best-of-3 templates: Coder-3B +7.60, Coder-7B +14.77, Base-7B +4.55. But template choice cuts Base-7B's apparent gain from +13.8 to +4.5, and third-party Coder-3B retrains score 16.1–21.4 vs 26.5 released ✓.
  - 2611.06965 shows that an executor key inherits the generator's false beliefs ✓.
  - SPICE's reward ablation ranks the AZR reward last (40.7 vs 44.9) ✓.

**R-Zero: Self-Evolving Reasoning LLM from Zero Data** ([2508.05004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05004), Aug 2025; ICLR 2026)
- *What it does.* A Challenger and a Solver are initialised from one base model and trained alternately with GRPO.
  - The Challenger is rewarded for Solver uncertainty, peaking at 50% agreement with the majority answer, minus a repetition penalty.
  - The Solver trains on majority-vote pseudo-labels, keeping only tasks where 3–7 of 10 answers match.
- *Setup.* Qwen3-4B/8B-Base and OctoThinker-3B/8B; 7 math and 4 general benchmarks; 3 iterations of 15 steps each.
- *Key results.*
  - Qwen3-4B math avg 42.57 → 49.93 ✓ (Table 1).
  - Untrained-Challenger baseline: 45.01 ✓.
  - Ablations: without filtering, general avg falls 31.15 → 26.69; without the repetition penalty, math falls 49.07 → 45.76 ✓.
  - **Collapse:** 0.6B peaks at step 15; 4B drops sharply at step 60 ✓ (§4.2).
  - **True pseudo-label accuracy** (GPT-4o oracle) falls 79% → 69% → 63% across iterations ✓ (Table 5).
- *Limitations / critiques.*
  - Internal inconsistencies:
    - the abstract says +6.49 on 4B math, but Table 1 implies +7.36;
    - the text says 8B goes 49.18 → 54.69, but Table 1 says 48.64 → 53.72 ✓.
  - R-Zero baselines differ across papers (Qwen3-8B AMC: 61.67 here vs 70.0 in SPICE's table) ✓.
  - R-Few shows 2-gram diversity falling from 35 to below 20 in 50 steps, then length inflation ✓.
  - Selection Is Not Learning: 42–52% of R-Zero/Agent0 admitted tasks leave the frontier under fresh samples, and R-Zero peaks at round 8 ✓.
  - R-Quest beats R-Zero by 17.32 at round 10, mostly because R-Zero collapses: R-Quest is only +5.75 over base ✓.

**SPADE: Self-Play in Adaptive Synthetic Executable Environments** ([2608.19197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19197), Aug 2026; ICLR'27 submission DJcwAmJV0T)
- *What it does.* One LLM plays two roles:
  - an **Environment Designer**, which writes complete stateful Gym-style environments as code (reset/step, reward, verifier), grounded on sampled DCLM documents and an environment memory;
  - a Reasoning Agent that learns in those environments.
  The Designer is trained on **hint-based regret**: the agent's return with privileged hints minus its return without them (PAIRED-like).
- *Setup.*
  - Qwen3-4B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-30B-A3B; 400 iterations.
  - Two settings, games and tool use; 8 held-out benchmarks (AIME'25/'26, GPQA-D, LCB-v6, Reasoning-Gym ×4).
  - Baselines: fixed-environment GRPO (offline pool written by the same backbone) and Fixed-env RLVE.
- *Key results.*
  - At 30B the average is 58.3: +8.1 over base and +5.3 over RLVE ✓ (Table 1).
  - At 4B and 8B the margin over RLVE is only +1.6 and +1.7 ✓.
  - BFCL v4 multi-turn +5.7; ACEBench-Agent +13.9 ✓.
  - Ablations at 30B (Table 3):
    - without memory: 53.2;
    - without corpus: 53.5;
    - **fixed GPT-5.5 designer: 53.0**;
    - without Designer training and memory: 40.5, i.e. 9.7 *below* the untrained model ✓.
  - The EMA learning-progress reward gives about 70% of the regret gain (55.9) ✓.
- *Limitations (authors).*
  - The "invisible leash": environment complexity is bounded by the base model.
  - The optimiser is fixed (GRPO).
  - No optimality proof; evaluation is on fixed tasks.
  - **Open:** "whether learned weights or in-context evolution makes the better designer" ✓.
- *Critique.*
  - Gains concentrate on Reasoning-Gym, which is closest in format to the games; competition math moves only +1–2.
  - The scale-dependent margin rests on one run per cell (no CIs seen).

**SPICE: Self-Play In Corpus Environments** ([2510.24684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24684), Oct 2025; Meta FAIR)
- *What it does.*
  - A Challenger mines documents (NaturalReasoning, Nemotron-CC-Math) and writes question–answer pairs whose answers are in a document the Reasoner never sees.
  - The Challenger's reward is Gaussian in the variance of the Reasoner's correctness (peak at p=0.5).
  - Shared weights, DrGRPO.
- *Setup.* Qwen3-4B/8B-Base and OctoThinker-3B/8B; 11 benchmarks.
- *Key results.*
  - Overall gain vs base: +9.1 / +5.7 / +10.5 / +11.9 ✓ (Table 1).
  - "Strong Challenger" baseline (fixed Qwen-32B-Instruct generator): +7.2 on 4B but only +2.6 on 8B ✓.
  - Without the corpus: 40.7 vs 43.9 ✓.
  - Reward ablation: AZR 40.7 < threshold 41.4 < R-Zero 43.6 < variance 44.9 ✓ (Table 4).
- *Limitations.*
  - The corpus acts as hidden supervision, so the paper is "zero human labels" rather than zero data.
  - Baselines are re-run, with numbers that differ from the original papers.
  - On 4B, a fixed 32B generator recovers about 80% of the gain.

**Agent0** ([2511.16043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16043), Nov 2025; COLM 2026)
- *What it does.* A curriculum agent and an executor agent share a base model and a Python tool.
  - The curriculum agent is rewarded for uncertainty, tool use and non-repetition.
  - The executor trains with ADPO, i.e. ambiguity-scaled GRPO, in multi-turn mode.
- *Setup.* Qwen3-4B/8B-Base; 3 iterations.
- *Key results.*
  - Qwen3-8B math avg 58.2, vs R-Zero 54.7, AZR 52.6, Socratic-Zero 56.1, and base with tool 53.2 ✓ (Table 1).
  - Per-iteration math: 55.1 → 56.5 → 58.2 ✓.
  - On tasks from iterations 1/2/3, the iteration-1 executor's pass rate is 64.0 / 58.5 / 51.0; tool calls per task go 1.65 → 2.60 ✓.
- *Limitations / critiques.*
  - The "w/o curriculum training" ablation (math 46.8) sits **below the untrained base with tool (53.2)**. That points to a confounded ablation rather than a measure of the curriculum's value ✓.
  - Selection Is Not Learning finds Agent0's inherited updates are often harmful, and EvoHeir beats Agent0 on 45 of 48 benchmark–backbone pairs ✓.

**Selection Is Not Learning: Choosing Model Futures (HeirCommit / EvoHeir)** ([2610.22029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22029), Oct 2026; MBZUAI)
- *What it does.* Names the **selection-to-update gap**: a task that looks learnable need not yield a useful update.
  - HeirCommit freezes each candidate successor and inherits it only if it beats the current model on fresh probes.
  - EvoHeir separates parameter inheritance from experience inheritance through an archive and repair step.
  - Regret is decomposed into selection, absorption and confirmation errors.
- *Setup.* Audits native updates of R-Zero, Agent0 and Skill-SP. 10 rounds × 3 seeds on Qwen3-4B/8B-Base, OLMo-3-7B and Gemma-4-E4B-it ✓.
- *Key results.*
  - 60 of 96 Skill-SP updates on verified tasks have non-positive correct-target NLL gain ✓.
  - 42–52% of admitted tasks leave the frontier under fresh responses ✓.
  - HeirCommit cuts the harmful share of commits by 68–83% vs random inheritance at the same acceptance rate ✓.
  - EvoHeir math avg is +6.0 / +7.0 / +6.8 / +1.2% relative over Agent0 ✓ (Table 1). Seed SD is about 0.4–0.7.
- *Limitations.*
  - Weight learners only.
  - Fresh probes cost extra compute, and that cost is not set against Agent0 at matched compute.
  - It still evaluates on fixed benchmarks.

**What Actually Evolves in Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning?** ([OpenReview ecw2VvTeE5](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ecw2VvTeE5), ICLR 2027 submission)
- *What it does.* Audits 6 self-evolving RL methods (R-Zero/AZR style) on 6 models and 7 benchmarks, against RL on human-annotated answers.
- *Key results* ◐:
  1. Most solver gains come from questions the base model already answers correctly.
  2. Self-evolving RL fixes far fewer errors than human-annotated RL.
  3. **Proposers do not teach more after further training.** The same solver trained on a later proposer's tasks does no better, and sometimes worse, than on an earlier proposer's tasks.
  4. A single-update theory says an update can only strengthen answers the model already gives.
- *Limitations.* I have the abstract only (PDF blocked). There is no per-skill steering statistic and no frozen-weight variant.

**When Can Self-Play Fix a False Belief?** ([2611.06965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06965), Nov 4 2026; AltSlate Labs)
- *What it does.* Expert-iteration self-play with Qwen3-4B-Base on output-prediction tasks. It tracks natural Python-semantics beliefs and two **planted** misreadings of task specifications.
- *Setup.* Runtime half: 4 arms × 3 rounds × 2 seeds. Spec half: 3 arms × 3 rounds × 2 planting strengths × 2 seeds. One GB10 GPU ✓ (§3.5).
- *Key results* ✓ (abstract, §1):
  - An executor key from the generator's own code encoded the "at most" misreading on every audited task, and the truth rate never rose above 0.03.
  - A key from an unplanted model raised it to 0.23–0.58.
  - The 1–7-of-8 **pass band admits almost no corrective tasks** while a belief is held.
  - Asked for corrective cases, the planted generator wrote 0 of 2,061 checkable tasks.
  - Fixes bring "replacement rules", such as overcounting the next value above a limit (0.18–0.38 of control samples).
- *Limitations.* A toy domain, a 4B model, 2 seeds. The sign test across 4 runs gives p=0.0625.

**Anchored Self-Play for Code Repair** ([2607.03523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03523), Jul 2026; ICML 2026, Stanford)
- *What it does.* Generator–fixer self-play for bug repair. Introduces BugSourceBench (human, human-edited LM, Qwen-7B and GPT-OSS-20B bugs). ASP adds an embedding-similarity reward toward a small reference set and mixes reference bugs into fixer batches.
- *Setup.* Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct; GRPO.
- *Key results* ✓ (Table 1a/b, §6–7):
  - Base 25.8, standard self-play 29.1, **ASP 36.1**.
  - Self-play degrades on Qwen-7B bugs (−1.5 pp) and drifts toward hard but unrealistic bugs. On realistic sources it improves early, then degrades.
  - Each anchoring component alone adds about 0.4–1.8; together they add 7.0.
  - Works with **as few as 50** reference bugs. The reference-pool composition steers which bug sources improve.
- *Limitations.* One domain and one model family. "Realism" is an embedding proxy.

**Guided Self-Evolving LLMs with Minimal Human Supervision (R-Few)** ([2512.02472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02472), Dec 2025; Tencent tech report)
- *What it does.* An R-Zero-style loop with two changes:
  - the Challenger is conditioned on 0–5 sampled human anchor examples from WebInstruct (1% or 5%);
  - the Solver trains on a mix of human and synthetic data under an online difficulty curriculum.
- *Key results* ✓ (Table 1, 2, Fig. 4):
  - Qwen3-8B avg: 55.1 (1%) / 56.7 (5%), vs R-Zero 53.7, AZR 52.8, **SPICE 55.4**, General-Reasoner 56.0.
  - On 4B, **SPICE (51.4) beats R-Few 1% (49.9) and 5% (50.7)**.
  - Without Challenger training: −1.9 math, −1.0 general.
  - R-Zero's diversity collapses and its questions inflate in length; R-Few's stay stable.
- *Limitations.* Not zero-data. On 4B, corpus grounding without labels does as well.

**Does the Reward Matter? A Placebo Trial for Zero-Data Self-Play** ([alphaXiv 2610.reward-placebo-zero-data-self-play](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.reward-placebo-zero-data-self-play), Oct 2026; single author, pre-registered, Zenodo)
- *What it does.* Holds base model (Qwen2.5-Coder-3B), a 100-step AZR seed-task stream and the optimiser fixed, and varies the reward:
  - a placebo that rewards correct tag format only;
  - AZR's execution-checked reward, with 2 seeds.
- *Key results* ✓:
  - Placebo vs base: −1.86 on 6 math benchmarks (95% CI [−4.85, 0.62]).
  - Correctness reward: +5.65 / +5.14.
  - Mean gain recovers about 72% of the released AZR gain (+7.54).
  - 93% of the gain is conditional correctness, not answer extraction.
- *Limitations.* No learned proposer, 100 steps, one model. The CIs do not capture training variance.
- *Implication.* AZR's code→math transfer is not just a format artefact. But it also shows that a **fixed seed stream with no proposer** gets about 72% of the gain.

**ActiveSaddler: Automated Curriculum Learning for Agent Harness Optimization** ([2610.00906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00906), Oct 2026; POSTECH/KAIST/MSR; ICLR'27 submission xmpyzuagpu)
- *What it does.*
  - Builds a non-stationary bandit whose arms are LLM-abstracted failure patterns.
  - Scores each arm by estimated learning progress, with explore/exploit over unseen scenarios.
  - Chooses which scenarios feed a **harness optimiser** (AutoSaddler, GEPA, Meta-Harness).
- *Key results.*
  - +4.4 pp on GAIA2 and +7.5 pp on TB2 over the same optimiser with a fixed shuffled order ✓.
  - +3.9 / +4.1 over difficulty-ordered fixed curricula ✓.
  - Reaches 58.5% dev accuracy at $298 vs $1,360 ✓.
- *Limitations.*
  - Selection only, no generation.
  - 2 optimisation runs; TB2 test has 40 tasks; LLM-judged arms. These are from the round-1 project review and are consistent with the text grep this session.

**OMNI-EPIC** ([2405.15568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15568), May 2024; ICLR 2025)
- *What it does.* A foundation model writes the next "learnable and interesting" task as environment code plus reward code. It keeps an archive of learned and failed tasks and uses a foundation-model success detector ✓ (abstract, §3).
- *Role in the field.*
  - It is the open-endedness anchor for "interestingness" as a third criterion beyond difficulty.
  - Every 2026 environment-writing paper (SPADE, LegoEnv, GameSmith) inherits its code-as-environment interface.
- *Limitations.* Qualitative; RL agents in simulation; no LLM-learner transfer study.

**Survey: Toward LLM Self-Play Under Data Scarcity** ([2611.08908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08908), Nov 6 2026; PKU/Shanghai AI Lab; a 6-page position survey)
- *Taxonomy.* Three paradigms:
  1. self-evolving (single-model self-training or adaptive learning);
  2. asymmetric co-evolution (proposer/solver, generator/discriminator);
  3. multi-agent evolution (competitive games, multi-role cooperation).
- *Three challenges and proposed directions:*
  - **Data-centric:** seed-bias amplification, marginal-utility and temporal-value decay of synthetic data. Proposed: "Health-Aware Data Allocation" and "Learnability-Aware Data Valuation".
  - **Generalizable:** diversity collapse and adaptive curricula in unverifiable domains. Proposed: "Generation–Evolution Pipeline" and "Causality-Aware Reward".
  - **Trustworthy:** noisy self-supervision, unsafe reasoning patterns, data poisoning. Proposed: "Behavioral Trustworthiness Regulation" and "Provenance-Grounded Trust Certification".
- *Small experiments* ✓:
  - Seed strategy changes proposer diversity: Vendi score 29.1 with dataset-as-seed vs 7.0–12.0 seed-free across GPT-5.5, Opus 4.7, Kimi K2 and DeepSeek-V4.
  - Rubrics raise judge robustness to length, format and tone hacking (e.g. DeepSeek-V3.2 0.36 → 0.75).
  - EasyRL iterations saturate after about round 3.
- *Value.* A useful vocabulary for the problems, not new evidence.

**Survey: Co-Evolution in Agentic Systems** ([2608.10299](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10299), Aug 2026; HKUST et al.)
- *Taxonomy.*
  - Stage 1, agent–agent: adversarial (pairwise or multi-source), collaborative, evolving organisations.
  - Stage 2, agent–environment: task space (exposure/selection vs adaptive generation), feedback space (preference, outcome, consistency), interaction space (executable world construction vs world models).
  - Stage 3, meta co-evolution: the evolution mechanism Ω is revised from a co-evolving system's joint trajectory, across what / when / how / where / evaluate.
- *Key claims* ✓:
  - A cross-paper figure shows Stage 1–2 gains "become smaller as evolution approaches a plateau".
  - Only RQGM (Iacob 2026) meets Stage 3. Gödel Agent, HyperAgents, PromptBreeder and MemEvolve are "single-entity precursors".
  - Calls for historical cross-play, component ablations and held-out evaluators.
- *Limitations (self-stated).* Safety is not operationalised.

**Survey sections used for framing** ✓:
- **Chen et al. 2607.07663 §4.4 and failure modes.**
  - Self-play is "the closest existing training paradigm to an autonomous curriculum".
  - Its central finding is stability: collapse is the default when the data gate or reward grounding fails (citing 2605.22217).
  - Failure modes are distributional collapse (the "entropy-reservoir" principle; Zenil's theorem that vanishing exogenous signal implies degeneration), **diversity collapse** ("novelty is a consumable resource that closed loops deplete") and **frame lock-in**.
  - Open question: "how little external grounding suffices … no one has established the exchange rate."
- **Duan et al. 2609.11873 §3.4.** Self-play and adaptive generation sit at L3. The valid / difficult / durable triad. "Difficulty-conditioned proposal offers partial rather than exact curriculum control" (PSV case).
- **Ren et al. 2607.13104.**
  - Self-generated tasks keep the signal extrinsic: the "agent may choose what to try, while the environment determines whether the attempt succeeds".
  - Game self-play "can create brittle competence", with strategic cycling. Calls for population-based, non-transitive evaluation.

### Consensus, debates & contradictions
1. **Consensus: closed loops degrade and grounding delays it.** Evidence:
   - R-Zero collapse, and pseudo-label accuracy falling 79→63% ✓;
   - R-Quest's invalid-question growth ✓;
   - SPICE's no-corpus drop of −3.2 ✓;
   - ASP drifting toward unrealistic bugs ✓;
   - Chen's entropy-reservoir and Zenil framing ✓.
   
   Every successful 2026 method imports exogenous signal: a corpus, anchors, an executor, a population, or fresh probes.
2. **Debate: does training the proposer matter?** For a small effect:
   - AZR: +1.4 ✓;
   - R-Few: +1.9 math ✓;
   - What Actually Evolves: later proposers do not teach more ◐;
   - DEO: a frozen generator with selection works ◐;
   - placebo trial: a fixed seed stream recovers 72% of AZR ✓.
   
   For a large effect:
   - Agent0: −11.4 without curriculum training, but that ablation falls below the base model ✓;
   - SPADE: removing Designer training and memory gives 40.5 vs 58.3 ✓, yet a fixed GPT-5.5 designer reaches 53.0 ✓.
   
   The honest summary is that the generator's *quality and grounding* matter. The *RL training of the proposer* is a modest, model-dependent add-on that has never been compared with "stronger fixed generator plus selection" at matched compute. SPICE's Strong Challenger comes closest (+7.2 vs +9.1 on 4B).
3. **Contradiction on which proposer signal is best.** Each paper's own ablation favours its own objective:
   - SPICE: variance > R-Zero uncertainty > threshold > AZR ✓;
   - SPADE: regret > EMA learning progress ✓;
   - CompassPlay: gradient alignment > difficulty ◐;
   - Dr. Free: no difficulty reward at all ◐;
   - DiagEvo: diagnosis > learnability ◐.
   
   There is no shared testbed, budget or seed protocol. Difficulty bands also *censor* corrective tasks: 2611.06965's pass band admits almost none ✓.
4. **Debate: sharpening vs new capability.**
   - For sharpening: What Actually Evolves (gains concentrate on already-solved items, a single update only sharpens) ◐; 2611.06965 (generator writes 0 of 2,061 corrective tasks) ✓.
   - Partly against: AZR keeps answer diversity at pass@k up to 512 ✓, and the placebo trial shows reward *content* matters ✓. Neither shows new competence.
5. **Benchmark numbers are not comparable across papers.**
   - R-Zero's own Qwen3-8B numbers disagree between abstract, text and table ✓.
   - Its AMC differs between R-Zero (61.67) and SPICE (70.0) ✓.
   - Bases differ by evaluation harness (Selection Is Not Learning's Qwen3-4B math base is 25.81; SPICE's is 35.8 overall) ✓.
   - Prompt templates can move AZR's gain from +13.8 to +4.5 ✓.
   
   Treat cross-paper SOTA claims of ±3 points as noise.
6. **Scale.** Gains and robustness grow with model size:
   - AZR +5.7 → +13.2 from 3B to 14B ✓;
   - R-Zero's collapse is delayed at 4B ✓;
   - SPADE's margin is +1.6 at 4B vs +5.3 at 30B ✓.
   
   But SPADE's "invisible leash" (environments bounded by the base model) and the sharpening audits suggest scale raises the ceiling without changing the mechanism.
7. **Harness learners.** Selection-based curricula help (ActiveSaddler ✓). Generation-based ones help with weak controls (HERA, DAEDALUS ◐). Adding data can hurt (SSR ◐). The UED learner-pathology result (2611.08787 ◐) has no text-space counterpart yet.

### Open problems
1. **Matched-compute proposer test.** Compare a trained proposer with a frozen strong generator + selection (DEO style) and with a fixed seed stream (placebo style), at equal FLOPs and solver rollouts, ≥3 seeds. No paper does all three.
2. **Out-of-loop "taught vs sharpened" ledger.** Per-item or per-rule accounting of which errors the curriculum *fixes* vs which correct answers it *sharpens*, against exact ground truth (What Actually Evolves, 2611.06965). Report it as a standard metric beside pass@1.
3. **Exchange rate for grounding.** Dose–response of corpus fraction, anchor count (ASP: 50 refs suffice; R-Few: 1–5%) and population size against rounds-to-collapse. 2607.07663 explicitly says this is unknown.
4. **Band censorship.** Learnability bands (1–7 of 8 correct) exclude the very tasks that would correct a confidently held error. Design and test band-free admission rules that still give gradient signal (2611.06965, Dr. Free).
5. **Inheritance gating vs task gating.** HeirCommit shows the inheritance decision matters more than the task decision. Its cost–benefit at matched compute, and its counterpart for harness learners, are untested.
6. **Unverifiable domains.** Judge co-adaptation (J-Zero, MAE, G-Zero) needs a placebo and a held-out-judge audit like the code→math trial. Co-cheating (False Frontiers) is the expected failure mode.
7. **Substrate swap (designer and learner).** SPADE's open question, "learned weights or in-context evolution makes the better designer", and its mirror: do regret, LP and variance teachers rank the same for harness learners as for weight learners?
8. **Open-endedness measurement.** Every paper evaluates on fixed benchmarks (SPADE limitation (c)). No accepted metric yet of novelty that keeps generating learnable signal; cf. OMNI-EPIC's archive and Zong's Stage-3 criterion.
9. **Learner trainability under self-curricula.** Plasticity loss and representational collapse in LLM learners under adaptive curricula (2611.08787 is RL-only), separated from proposer collapse.
10. **Safety of closed loops.** AZR's "uh-oh moment", Yu et al.'s unsafe-pattern amplification, and poisoning of seeds and corpora. There are no measured rates, only anecdotes.

### Where it is heading (to mid-2027)
- **More audits than methods.** Expect ICLR rebuttal revisions of What Actually Evolves and 2611.06965 that add per-category or yoked controls. Expect ICML'27 versions with proposer-swap and replay controls. The methods lane (Zero-variants per domain: SQL-Zero, Tempo-Zero, Audio-Zero, MTA-Zero, Explorer-Zero) will keep growing, with diminishing novelty. Our field forecast (round2/field_forecast.md) calls it "saturated for methods".
- **Environments as code become the default unit.** SPADE, GameSmith, LegoEnv, EnvFactory and Learning-to-Build-the-Environment point toward reusable, verifiable environments with memory and corpus grounding, scaled to 30B+.
- **Grounding and gating become standard parts of the recipe.** A corpus or anchor set, validity and novelty filters (R-Quest, Prism), and fresh-probe inheritance gates (HeirCommit) will be expected baselines. Proposer RL may become optional (DEO, Strong Challenger).
- **Move into unverifiable and agentic domains.** Judge co-evolution (J-Zero), rubric curricula, and search or GUI agents (VISTA, Dr. Free, False Frontiers), with co-cheating as the central risk.
- **Frozen-weight curricula.** Harness and memory learners whose curriculum writer is itself an editable artifact (RoboACE, 2611.23219, HERA, ActiveSaddler follow-ups). This is the Stage-3 "meta co-evolution" Zong says is nearly empty.
- **Scaling claims.** Self-Play Pretraining's compute-scaling curve is the first "scaling law" claim for self-play. Expect follow-ups to test it on natural-language-structured generators.

### Connection to our ideas
- **Blind-Spot Hiding / curriculum-Goodhart** (round-2 #5; FINAL_REPORT): *directly affected.*
  - The weight-side phenomenon is now well established: What Actually Evolves ◐, 2611.06965 (0 of 2,061 corrective tasks; pass-band censorship) ✓, Anchored Self-Play drift ✓, R-Few and R-Quest diversity and validity decay ✓.
  - What remains novel is narrow: a **frozen-weight meta-agent editing its own task writer**, a **signed per-rule steering statistic against exact truth**, and a **yoked twin**.
  - Must-cite list: ecw2VvTeE5, 2611.06965, 2610.22029, ASP, R-Quest, ActiveSaddler (as a named arm), CompassPlay, DEO.
  - 2611.06965 is closer than the round-2 sweep implied and should be re-read in full before any write-up.
- **#1 Taught, Not Selected:** 2611.06965 is a weight-level example of the "reading channel". The answer key inherits the generator's false belief, and the loop cannot remove it without a decorrelated key. False Frontiers' co-cheating is the score-channel counterpart. Both are strong motivating citations. Neither tests reflective (text) optimisers.
- **#3 Gate, Not Willingness, and the 2+3 merge:** Selection Is Not Learning is the closest weight-side analogue of "where improvement dies". Task admission (proposal), update absorption, and confirmation map onto our propose / propagate / pay-off funnel. HeirCommit's fresh-probe gate is a ready-made gate arm. The 68–83% harmful-commit reduction ✓ is a citable prior for "the gate matters".
- **#5 Order-0 Rewards Breed Routers:** the proposer-reward debate is the same question one level down. Difficulty and variance rewards are *order-0 proxies* (solver pass rate on the task). CompassPlay's "where it moves the solver" and HeirCommit's realized-transition test are *order-1-style* rewards. What Actually Evolves (later proposers teach no more) is evidence that order-0 proposer rewards do not produce better teachers. This gives #5 a cross-substrate framing.
- **#7 Varying Goals and #9 MetaShift:** OMNI-EPIC, SPADE and LegoEnv are ready-made sources of *changing worlds*, i.e. procedurally varying goals. 2611.23219 shows a frozen agent can be left to write its own practice games. Use a SPADE/OMNI-style generator as the "goal-switching" schedule. Be careful: self-written worlds drift toward what the agent can do (ASP, R-Few), so a fixed external schedule is the cleaner manipulation.
- **#8 Openness Ladder:** SPADE's "invisible leash" (designer complexity bounded by model scale) and its scale-dependent margin (+1.6 at 4B vs +5.3 at 30B) ✓ support the claim that optimal openness depends on model strength. The writable surface here is the environment, not the harness.
- **#6 Meta-Overfitting:** ASP's reference-pool composition steers which bug sources improve ✓. This is a clean weight-side example of curricula specialising to "home" distributions, useful as related work.
- **Instrumentation shared across ideas:**
  - SPICE's and SPADE's corpus grounding and environment memory, plus GRADUS-style guarded operators (round-1), give exact-truth task worlds.
  - The placebo-trial design (fixed stream, vary only the reward, pre-registered, paired bootstrap) is the template for our matched controls.
